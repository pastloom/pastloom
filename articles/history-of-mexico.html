History of Mexico

This article is written in English. Choose a language to open an automatic translation by Google Translate. Automatic translations can sometimes get historical names and dates wrong — for full accuracy, read the original English article.

Mexico's story begins with some of the greatest ancient civilisations of the Americas — the Olmec, the Maya and the Aztec — who built cities, pyramids and calendars long before Europeans arrived.

Spain conquered the Aztec Empire in 1519–1521 and ruled Mexico for 300 years. Mexico won independence in 1821, and its great revolution of 1910 reshaped the country again.

Ancient Mexico

The Olmec, sometimes called the mother culture of Mesoamerica, carved giant stone heads more than 3,000 years ago. The Maya built great cities in the jungle and became expert astronomers with an accurate calendar.

The Aztec built their capital Tenochtitlan on a lake, with canals, markets and temples. When the Spanish first saw it in 1519, it was one of the largest cities in the world.

New Spain

Cortés and his allies defeated the Aztec Empire by 1521. Mexico became the colony of New Spain, ruled from Mexico City, built on the ruins of Tenochtitlan.

Spanish priests spread Christianity, silver mines made the colony rich, and a new mixed culture — mestizo — grew from Indigenous, Spanish and African peoples. But disease and hard labour killed millions of Indigenous people.

Independence and revolution

In 1810 the priest Miguel Hidalgo called for freedom, and after eleven years of war Mexico became independent in 1821. The young country lost almost half its land to the United States in 1848 and fought off a French invasion in the 1860s.

In 1910 revolution broke out against the long rule of Porfirio Díaz. Leaders such as Emiliano Zapata demanded land for the poor. The revolution's promises — land, education and workers' rights — shaped modern Mexico.

Did You Know?

Chocolate, tomatoes, maize (corn) and chillies all came to the rest of the world from ancient Mexico.
