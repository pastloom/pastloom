Vijayanagara Empire

This article is written in English. Choose a language to open an automatic translation by Google Translate. Automatic translations can sometimes get historical names and dates wrong — for full accuracy, read the original English article.

The Vijayanagara Empire was the last great Hindu empire of South India. It was founded in 1336 by two brothers, Harihara and Bukka, and its capital, Vijayanagara — the 'City of Victory' — became one of the largest cities in the world.

For over 200 years the empire protected South India's temples, trade and art, until a great defeat in 1565 left its magnificent capital in ruins. Those ruins, at Hampi, are now a World Heritage Site.

The City of Victory

Harihara and Bukka built their capital on the rocky banks of the Tungabhadra River, among giant granite boulders. Foreign travellers said Vijayanagara was as large as Rome, with rich markets selling gems, horses, silk and spices.

At its peak the empire ruled almost all of South India, from coast to coast, and its kings built and repaired thousands of temples.

Krishnadevaraya, the golden age

Krishnadevaraya, who ruled from 1509 to 1529, was the empire's greatest king — a victorious general, a builder of great temples and tanks, and himself a poet.

His court welcomed poets in many languages, and Portuguese traders came to buy horses and sell goods. Travellers wrote with wonder about the city's festivals, jewels and crowded bazaars.

Talikota and the ruins of Hampi

In 1565 the allied Deccan sultanates defeated Vijayanagara at the Battle of Talikota. The capital was looted and abandoned, and the empire shrank to a shadow in the south before ending in 1646.

Today the stone temples, elephant stables and royal baths of Hampi stand among the boulders — silent proof of how grand the City of Victory once was.

Did You Know?

A Portuguese traveller wrote that in Vijayanagara's markets you could buy rubies, diamonds and pearls in great heaps — and the city may have held around 500,000 people.
