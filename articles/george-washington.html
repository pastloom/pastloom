George Washington

This article is written in English. Choose a language to open an automatic translation by Google Translate. Automatic translations can sometimes get historical names and dates wrong — for full accuracy, read the original English article.

George Washington was the general who led America's army to victory against Britain, and then became the first President of the United States.

Twice he gave up power when he could have kept it — first his army command, then the presidency after two terms. That example helped shape democracy in America and beyond.

The general who would not give up

Washington was a Virginia landowner and surveyor who had fought for Britain as a young officer. When the American colonies rebelled in 1775, Congress chose him to command their new army.

His army was often outnumbered, poorly fed and close to falling apart. His Christmas-night crossing of the icy Delaware River in 1776 to win at Trenton saved the cause — and his greatest skill was keeping the army alive until France joined the war and victory came at Yorktown in 1781.

The power he handed back

When the war ended, Washington did something that astonished kings: he resigned his command and went home to his farm. People had expected a victorious general to seize power; Washington refused.

In 1787 he led the meeting that wrote the United States Constitution, and in 1789 he was unanimously elected the first President. Everything he did — from calling himself 'Mr President' to serving only two terms — set patterns that American presidents still follow.

A complicated legacy

Washington's Farewell Address warned his young country against permanent alliances and bitter party divisions. He retired in 1797 and died two years later at his home, Mount Vernon.

His record has a darker side: Washington enslaved people at Mount Vernon throughout his life, though his will freed those he owned outright after his wife's death. He is honoured as the father of his country — a leader whose self-control built a republic, in a nation still struggling to live up to its promise of liberty for all.

Did You Know?

Washington is the only American president who was elected with every single vote of the Electoral College — and it happened twice, in 1789 and 1792.
