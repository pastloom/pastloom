Wu Zetian

This article is written in English. Choose a language to open an automatic translation by Google Translate. Automatic translations can sometimes get historical names and dates wrong — for full accuracy, read the original English article.

Wu Zetian was the only woman in Chinese history to rule as emperor in her own name. She rose from being a palace attendant to ruling the Tang dynasty, first through her husband and sons, and then openly as emperor from 690 to 705.

She was a skilled and strict ruler. She chose officials by examination and talent, strengthened the empire, and supported Buddhism — but she also punished rivals harshly.

A rise through the palace

Wu entered the palace as a young attendant of Emperor Taizong. After his death she returned as the favourite, and then empress, of the next emperor, Gaozong.

When Gaozong fell ill, Wu handled much of the government. After his death she ruled through her sons, and in 690 she took the throne herself — a step no Chinese woman had taken before.

Ruling the empire

Wu expanded the civil service examinations, so officials could be chosen for ability rather than family connections. She promoted able people, lowered some taxes and kept the empire stable and prosperous.

She made Luoyang her capital and raised great Buddhist monuments, including work at the Longmen Grottoes. Her giant statue there is said to bear her face.

Her final years and memory

In 705, elderly and ill, Wu was forced to give the throne back to her son, restoring the Tang dynasty. She died later that year.

Historians have judged her in very different ways — some praise her talent and strong rule, others stress her cruelty to rivals. Her blank tomb tablet near Xi'an, with no words carved on it, still sparks debate about how she should be remembered.

Did You Know?

Wu Zetian's tomb tablet was left completely blank — some say she wanted later generations to judge her deeds for themselves.
