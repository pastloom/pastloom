Cleopatra

This article is written in English. Choose a language to open an automatic translation by Google Translate. Automatic translations can sometimes get historical names and dates wrong — for full accuracy, read the original English article.

Cleopatra VII was the last ruler of ancient Egypt. She was famous for her intelligence, her languages, and her alliances with two of Rome's most powerful men, Julius Caesar and Mark Antony.

She fought to keep Egypt free and rich while Rome grew stronger around it. When she died in 30 BCE, Egypt became a Roman province and the age of the pharaohs ended.

The last pharaoh

Cleopatra came from a Greek family, the Ptolemies, who had ruled Egypt for almost 300 years. She became queen at 18, sharing the throne first with her father, then her young brother.

Unlike earlier rulers of her family, she learned the Egyptian language and presented herself as an Egyptian queen, which made her popular with her people.

Caesar and Antony

When Julius Caesar came to Egypt, Cleopatra allied with him and kept her throne. After Caesar was murdered in Rome, she joined Mark Antony, one of Rome's new leaders.

Together, Cleopatra and Antony tried to build a power in the east to match Rome. Antony's rival, Octavian, declared war, and their forces were defeated at the Battle of Actium in 31 BCE.

The end of an age

In 30 BCE, as Octavian's army reached Alexandria, Antony and then Cleopatra took their own lives. The famous story that she died from the bite of a snake is a legend that may or may not be true.

Egypt became part of the Roman Empire, and Cleopatra became the last pharaoh. Her life is still retold in plays, films and books after 2,000 years.

Did You Know?

Cleopatra lived closer in time to the iPhone than to the building of the Great Pyramid of Egypt.
