The Dome of the Rock

This article is written in English. Choose a language to open an automatic translation by Google Translate. Automatic translations can sometimes get historical names and dates wrong — for full accuracy, read the original English article.

The Dome of the Rock is a shrine in Jerusalem with a shining golden dome. It was finished in 691–692 CE and is the oldest surviving monument of Islamic architecture.

It stands on a raised platform in the Old City that is holy in Judaism, Christianity and Islam. For more than 1,300 years its dome has watched over the city, through caliphs, Crusaders, sultans and modern times.

An Umayyad masterpiece

Caliph Abd al-Malik of the Umayyad dynasty ordered the shrine at the end of the 600s. Craftsmen covered its walls with glass mosaics in green and gold — trees, jewels and flowing patterns, but no living figures.

Inside, an octagonal walkway circles the rock at the centre. Later rulers repaired and enriched the building; the famous blue tiles on its outer walls were added under the Ottoman sultan Suleiman in the 1500s.

A place holy to three faiths

The platform beneath the shrine — called the Temple Mount by Jews and Christians and Haram al-Sharif by Muslims — has been a holy place for thousands of years. Jewish temples stood there in ancient times, and the site holds deep meaning in Islamic tradition as well.

Because the place matters so much to so many, it has been cared for, fought over and protected across the centuries — and remains one of the most sensitive and treasured sites on Earth.

Through the centuries

Crusaders who took Jerusalem in 1099 turned the shrine into a church; after Saladin retook the city in 1187, it returned to Muslim care. Ottoman sultans maintained it for 400 years.

The dome's covering has been renewed many times — most recently regilded in the 1990s with support from King Hussein of Jordan. Golden above Jerusalem's stone streets, it is still the city's most famous sight.

Did You Know?

The Dome of the Rock is not a mosque — it is a shrine. The silver-domed al-Aqsa Mosque, where people gather for prayers, stands nearby on the same platform.
