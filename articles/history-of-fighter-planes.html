History of Fighter Planes

This article is written in English. Choose a language to open an automatic translation by Google Translate. Automatic translations can sometimes get historical names and dates wrong — for full accuracy, read the original English article.

Fighter planes are warplanes built to fight other planes in the sky. They appeared in the First World War, when pilots first shot at each other above the trenches.

From wooden biplanes with hand-thrown bricks to jets faster than sound, fighters have always pushed flying technology to its limits.

Duels above the trenches

Early war pilots flew on scouting missions, waving at enemy flyers. Soon they carried pistols and rifles, then machine guns. In 1915, a gear that let a gun fire safely through a spinning propeller created the first true fighters.

Skilled pilots became 'aces' and national heroes. The German ace Manfred von Richthofen, the Red Baron, flew bright red planes and won 80 air battles before he was killed in 1918.

Spitfires and the first jets

In 1940, Britain's Spitfires and Hurricanes fought Germany's Messerschmitts in the Battle of Britain — the first great battle fought only in the air. Radar guided the defenders, and the fighters saved Britain from invasion.

Late in the Second World War, Germany flew the Me 262, the first jet fighter. It was far faster than propeller planes, but it arrived too late and in too few numbers to change the war.

Faster than sound

Jets took over after 1945. Over Korea, American Sabres and Soviet-built MiGs fought the first jet-versus-jet battles in a stretch of sky nicknamed MiG Alley.

Later fighters added radar, guided missiles and computers. Today's stealth fighters are built to be nearly invisible to radar — a long way from canvas wings and a pilot's sharp eyes.

Did You Know?

The Red Baron's flying circus painted its planes in bright colours — the opposite of camouflage — so enemies would know, and fear, exactly who was coming.
