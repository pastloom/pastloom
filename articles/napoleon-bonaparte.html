Napoleon Bonaparte

This article is written in English. Choose a language to open an automatic translation by Google Translate. Automatic translations can sometimes get historical names and dates wrong — for full accuracy, read the original English article.

Napoleon Bonaparte was a French general who became Emperor of France. He was one of the greatest military leaders in history, and his armies marched across almost all of Europe.

He also changed France itself, with new laws that are still used today. In the end he was defeated at the Battle of Waterloo in 1815 and sent away to a lonely island.

From soldier to emperor

Napoleon was born on Corsica, a French island in the Mediterranean Sea. He trained as an army officer and rose very fast during the French Revolution because he won battles that others lost.

In 1799 he seized power in France. Five years later, in 1804, he crowned himself Emperor of the French.

Wars across Europe

For more than ten years, Napoleon's armies fought the other powers of Europe. He won famous victories such as Austerlitz in 1805 and Jena in 1806, and at his height he controlled or influenced most of mainland Europe.

His laws, the Napoleonic Code, gave all men equal rights before the law in the lands he ruled. Parts of that code are still the basis of law in many countries today.

Defeat and exile

In 1812 Napoleon invaded Russia with a huge army. The bitter winter and long distances destroyed most of it, and his power began to break.

He was exiled to the island of Elba, escaped, and ruled France again for one hundred days. After his final defeat at Waterloo in 1815, he was sent to Saint Helena in the far Atlantic, where he died in 1821.

Did You Know?

Napoleon was not unusually short. The idea comes from old British cartoons and a mix-up between French and English inches.
