History of Sleep

This article is written in English. Choose a language to open an automatic translation by Google Translate. Automatic translations can sometimes get historical names and dates wrong — for full accuracy, read the original English article.

Sleep takes about a third of every human life, but how people sleep has changed a lot through history. Before cheap lamps and electric light, most people went to bed soon after dark — and many woke in the middle of the night for an hour or more before sleeping again.

Today scientists study sleep with machines that watch the brain, and they have found that this nightly rest repairs the body and builds our memories.

Sleeping with the sun

For thousands of years, darkness ruled the night. Candles and oil lamps were costly, so most families went to bed early and rose with the light. Whole families often shared one bed, and travellers sometimes shared with strangers at inns.

Because the night was long, many people naturally woke around midnight. This quiet waking hour was used for prayer, talk, crafts and snacks — then people returned to bed for their second sleep until morning.

How one long sleep took over

Street lamps, gaslight and then electric bulbs pushed bedtime later and later. Factory hours and alarm clocks squeezed sleep into a single block, and the old two-sleep pattern faded from memory.

By the 1900s, sleeping eight hours in one stretch was called natural — though it was really a new habit made by light and work timetables.

What science found in the night

In the 1900s, researchers recorded the brain during sleep and found it is not switched off at all. The brain cycles through light sleep, deep sleep and dream sleep all night long.

Today doctors know that poor sleep harms memory, mood and health. The old advice — keep regular hours and a dark, quiet room — turns out to be very modern science.

Did You Know?

Before alarm clocks, British and Irish 'knocker-uppers' earned money by tapping workers' windows with long poles or shooting peas at the glass to wake them for the factory.
