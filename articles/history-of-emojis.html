History of Emojis

This article is written in English. Choose a language to open an automatic translation by Google Translate. Automatic translations can sometimes get historical names and dates wrong — for full accuracy, read the original English article.

Emojis — the small pictures we tap into messages — are the newest chapter in the history of writing, and in some ways the oldest: like Egyptian hieroglyphs, they are tiny pictures that carry meaning.

They began in Japan in 1999 with 176 simple icons on mobile phones. Today there are more than 3,700 emojis, used billions of times a day in almost every language on Earth.

Faces made of letters

Long before picture keyboards, people made faces from type. In 1881 an American humour magazine printed four typographic faces showing joy, sadness, indifference and astonishment.

On early internet message boards in 1982, Scott Fahlman proposed :-) and :-( to mark jokes and serious posts. In Japan, users built richer faces from characters, like (^_^), that read upright instead of sideways.

Japan invents the emoji

In 1999, designer Shigetaka Kurita created 176 tiny pictures for Japanese mobile phones — hearts, weather, food, faces and signs, each just 12 by 12 dots. The word emoji comes from Japanese for 'picture character'.

Other phone companies copied the idea, and in 2010 emoji were added to Unicode, the world standard for text. When Apple and Android put emoji keyboards on phones worldwide, the little pictures exploded.

A new picture language

Emojis now soften jokes, replace words and cross language barriers — a thumbs-up or a heart needs no translation. New emojis are approved every year after public proposals.

They also carry history forward: writing began with pictures, became letters, and has now welcomed pictures back. Linguists note emojis are not a full language — but they are the fastest-spreading addition to writing ever seen.

Did You Know?

July 17 is World Emoji Day — because that is the date shown on Apple's calendar emoji, which marks the day Apple's calendar app was launched in 2002.
