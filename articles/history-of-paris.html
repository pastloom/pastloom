History of Paris

This article is written in English. Choose a language to open an automatic translation by Google Translate. Automatic translations can sometimes get historical names and dates wrong — for full accuracy, read the original English article.

Paris, the capital of France, began more than 2,000 years ago as a small settlement of the Parisii tribe on an island in the River Seine. It grew into one of the most famous cities in the world — a centre of art, fashion, philosophy and revolution.

Paris is often called the City of Light, for its early street lamps and for the bright ideas of its thinkers. About two million people live in the city itself, and more than ten million in the area around it.

From Lutetia to a royal capital

The Romans conquered the Parisii settlement and built a town called Lutetia, with baths, temples and an arena. In the 500s, King Clovis of the Franks made Paris his capital.

In the Middle Ages Paris became one of Europe's greatest cities. Work began on Notre-Dame Cathedral in 1163, the University of Paris drew students from every land, and strong walls protected a busy trading city of merchants and craftsmen.

Revolution and a rebuilt city

In 1789 the French Revolution began in Paris, when crowds stormed the Bastille prison. Kings fell, a republic was proclaimed, and the ideas of liberty and equality spread across Europe — along with years of fear and violence.

In the 1850s and 1860s, Baron Haussmann rebuilt Paris for Emperor Napoleon III: wide boulevards, parks and elegant stone buildings replaced the crowded old streets. In 1889 the Eiffel Tower rose for a world fair, and though many artists hated it at first, it became the symbol of the city.

Paris today

German armies occupied Paris in the Second World War, from 1940 until the city was freed in August 1944. After the war, Paris grew again as a centre of art, fashion, food and film.

Today millions of visitors come every year for its museums, galleries and cafes. The fire that damaged Notre-Dame in 2019 shocked the world — and the great cathedral was carefully restored and reopened, as Paris has rebuilt itself so many times before.

Did You Know?

The Eiffel Tower was only meant to stand for 20 years. It was saved because its height made it useful as a radio tower.
