History of Lahore

This article is written in English. Choose a language to open an automatic translation by Google Translate. Automatic translations can sometimes get historical names and dates wrong — for full accuracy, read the original English article.

Lahore, the great city of Pakistan's Punjab province, stands near the River Ravi. It is more than a thousand years old and is loved as the cultural heart of Pakistan — a city of gardens, poetry, food and music.

Lahore reached its greatest glory under the Mughal emperors, who filled it with forts, mosques and gardens. An old saying in the region declares that whoever has not seen Lahore has not yet been born.

The early city

Lahore's beginnings are wrapped in legend, which names it after Loh, a son of the hero Rama. The first firm records show a fortified town by about the 1000s CE, and in the 1000s the Ghaznavid sultans made it an important city of their empire — a gateway between Central Asia and India.

For centuries Lahore was ruled in turn by sultans of Delhi and local governors. Babur captured it in 1524 on his way to founding the Mughal Empire, and under the Mughals the city's greatest age began.

The Mughal jewel

The emperor Akbar made Lahore a capital from 1584 to 1598 and rebuilt its great fort in brick and stone. His son Jahangir loved the city and is buried there in a beautiful tomb by the Ravi.

Shah Jahan, born in Lahore, added marble palaces to the fort and laid out the Shalimar Gardens. In 1673 Aurangzeb built the Badshahi Mosque, one of the largest mosques in the world, facing the fort across a great courtyard. Poets, painters and Sufi saints made Lahore a city of learning and faith.

Sikhs, British and Pakistan

After Mughal power faded, Lahore became in 1799 the capital of Maharaja Ranjit Singh's Sikh Empire, which ruled Punjab until the British annexed it in 1849. Under British rule Lahore gained a university, museums, railway stations and fine new buildings in a mixed Mughal-Gothic style.

In 1940 the Lahore Resolution was passed here, calling for separate Muslim homelands — the key step towards the creation of Pakistan in 1947, when Lahore became a Pakistani city. Today it is Pakistan's cultural capital, famous for its gardens, its historic walled city and its food streets.

Did You Know?

The Badshahi Mosque in Lahore, built in only about two years (1671–1673), could hold around 100,000 worshippers in its vast courtyard.
