History of Korea

This article is written in English. Choose a language to open an automatic translation by Google Translate. Automatic translations can sometimes get historical names and dates wrong — for full accuracy, read the original English article.

Korea is a peninsula in East Asia, between China and Japan. People have lived there for tens of thousands of years, and its recorded history goes back more than 2,000 years.

For most of that history Korea was a united kingdom with its own language, writing and culture. Only in the last century was it divided into North Korea and South Korea.

Kingdoms of the peninsula

Legend says the first Korean kingdom, Gojoseon, was founded in very ancient times. By the early centuries CE, three kingdoms — Goguryeo, Baekje and Silla — shared the peninsula.

Silla united most of Korea in 676. Later the Goryeo kingdom gave Korea its modern name, and the long Joseon kingdom, founded in 1392, ruled for more than 500 years.

A culture of its own

King Sejong the Great gave Korea its own alphabet, Hangul, in the 1440s — one of the most scientific writing systems ever made, designed so ordinary people could learn to read.

Korean craftspeople made beautiful celadon pottery, printed books with metal movable type before Europe, and built turtle ships — armoured warships that helped defeat Japanese invasions in the 1590s.

Division and two Koreas

Japan ruled Korea from 1910 until the end of World War II in 1945. The peninsula was then divided between Soviet and American zones, becoming two states in 1948.

War between them followed in 1950. Since 1953 an uneasy peace has held, and the two Koreas remain divided, though their people share one language and a long common history.

Did You Know?

Korea printed a book with metal movable type in 1377 — about 78 years before Gutenberg's famous Bible in Europe.
