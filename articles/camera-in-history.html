The Camera in History

This article is written in English. Choose a language to open an automatic translation by Google Translate. Automatic translations can sometimes get historical names and dates wrong — for full accuracy, read the original English article.

The camera captures a moment and keeps it forever. Before it, the only way to remember a face or a place was a painting or a drawing.

The first lasting photograph was made in 1826 by Joseph Nicéphore Niépce, after an eight-hour exposure. Within a century, cameras were in the hands of ordinary people — and today almost everyone carries one in a phone.

The first photograph

Niépce coated a metal plate with a light-sensitive substance and set it in a camera pointed at his rooftops. After hours in sunlight, a faint image appeared — the first photograph.

In 1839, Louis Daguerre announced a faster method, the daguerreotype. Suddenly portraits, which once took a painter days, could be made in minutes.

Portraits for everyone

Photography studios opened in every city. Families could own pictures of their loved ones for the first time, and newspapers began to print photographs.

In 1888, Kodak sold a simple box camera with the slogan 'You press the button, we do the rest.' Anyone could now take photographs.

Film, colour and digital

Moving pictures — film — grew from photography in the 1890s. Colour film arrived in the early 1900s, and digital cameras in the 1990s replaced film with sensors.

Today a phone camera can take a sharp photo in a fraction of a second and share it around the world in moments.

Did You Know?

The first photograph took about eight hours to make — today a phone camera takes one in a fraction of a second.
