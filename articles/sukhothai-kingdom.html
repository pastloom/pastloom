The Sukhothai Kingdom

This article is written in English. Choose a language to open an automatic translation by Google Translate. Automatic translations can sometimes get historical names and dates wrong — for full accuracy, read the original English article.

The Sukhothai Kingdom was the first great kingdom of the Thai people, in what is now Thailand. It began in 1238, when Thai chiefs broke free from the Khmer Empire.

Under King Ramkhamhaeng the Great, Sukhothai grew rich and peaceful. He is remembered for creating the Thai writing system and ruling his people like a kind father.

The first Thai kingdom

For centuries the Thai lands were ruled by the Khmer Empire from Angkor. In 1238 two Thai chiefs joined together, defeated the Khmer governor of Sukhothai, and set up an independent kingdom.

The new kingdom grew quickly along the river valleys, where farmers grew rice in the rich wet fields.

Ramkhamhaeng the Great

Sukhothai's third king, Ramkhamhaeng, ruled from about 1279 to 1298 and led the kingdom to its golden age. Under him Sukhothai stretched over much of today's Thailand.

A famous stone inscription from his reign says he created the Thai alphabet in 1283. It also describes a bell hung at the palace gate: any person with a complaint could ring it, and the king himself would come and judge fairly.

Joining Ayutthaya

After Ramkhamhaeng, weaker kings followed, and the rising kingdom of Ayutthaya to the south slowly took over Sukhothai's lands.

In 1438 Sukhothai was fully absorbed into Ayutthaya. Its graceful Buddha statues and ruined temples still stand in the Sukhothai Historical Park, loved by visitors today.

Did You Know?

Ramkhamhaeng's stone inscription is one of the oldest writings in the Thai language — and it describes a land so rich that there were 'fish in the water and rice in the fields'.
