History of Literature

This article is written in English. Choose a language to open an automatic translation by Google Translate. Automatic translations can sometimes get historical names and dates wrong — for full accuracy, read the original English article.

Literature means the stories, poems and plays that people write down and treasure. It began with spoken tales told around fires, long before anyone could write.

The oldest literature we still have is more than 4,000 years old — and people are still writing new books today, in hundreds of languages, adding to the same great river of stories.

Stories before books

Every people had storytellers who carried the memory of the tribe: tales of gods, heroes, tricksters and creation. Singers performed long poems from memory, sometimes for days.

When writing appeared, the old tales were written down. In Mesopotamia, scribes pressed the story of King Gilgamesh into clay tablets. In India, the great Sanskrit epics were composed, and in Greece, Homer's Iliad and Odyssey told of the Trojan War and a hero's long journey home.

Poets, plays and holy books

Literature flowered in many lands at once: Chinese poets like Li Bai, Persian poets like Rumi, and Arabic storytellers who gathered the tales of the Thousand and One Nights.

In Europe, wandering poets sang of knights and love, monks copied books by hand, and playwrights like Shakespeare filled theatres with plays that are still performed 400 years later.

Printing and the world of readers

Gutenberg's printing press changed everything. Books poured out in thousands, spelling settled down, and reading stopped being a skill of priests and kings alone.

In the last few centuries, literature has spread to every country and language. Novels, poems and plays now record not only kings and heroes, but farmers, workers, children and everyday life — everyone's story can be told.

Did You Know?

The Epic of Gilgamesh, written on clay tablets in Mesopotamia over 4,000 years ago, includes a story of a great flood — one of the oldest flood stories in the world.
