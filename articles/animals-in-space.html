Animals in Space

This article is written in English. Choose a language to open an automatic translation by Google Translate. Automatic translations can sometimes get historical names and dates wrong — for full accuracy, read the original English article.

Before humans flew into space, animals went first. Scientists sent fruit flies, dogs, monkeys and other animals on rockets to learn whether living things could survive the journey.

The most famous is Laika, a dog from Moscow who became the first living creature to orbit the Earth in 1957. These animal pioneers helped open the way for human spaceflight.

Why animals went first

No one knew if a living body could survive a rocket launch, weightlessness and radiation. Small animals — flies, mice and monkeys — were sent up to find out.

Their flights measured heartbeats and breathing in space, and each safe return gave scientists confidence to go further.

Laika and the space dogs

The Soviet Union chose dogs, which were patient and used to cold and hunger. Laika, a gentle stray from Moscow's streets, orbited Earth in 1957 — but her spacecraft could not bring her back, and she died in orbit.

In 1960, Belka and Strelka became the first animals to orbit Earth and return alive. Strelka later had puppies, and one was given to the family of the American president.

Opening the door for humans

In 1961 the chimpanzee Ham flew into space and performed simple tasks, proving a pilot could work in weightlessness. Months later, Yuri Gagarin became the first human in space.

Animals still travel to space stations today for science. And Laika is remembered with statues in Moscow — the little dog who went first.

Did You Know?

Strelka, one of the first dogs to return safely from orbit, later had puppies — and one puppy was given as a gift to the family of US President John F. Kennedy.
