Pyramids of Giza

This article is written in English. Choose a language to open an automatic translation by Google Translate. Automatic translations can sometimes get historical names and dates wrong — for full accuracy, read the original English article.

The Pyramids of Giza stand on a rocky plateau near Cairo, in Egypt. They were built about 4,500 years ago as royal tombs for three pharaohs.

The largest, the Great Pyramid, was the tallest building on Earth for more than 3,800 years. The pyramids are the only wonder of the ancient world that still stands today.

Three pyramids on the plateau

The first and largest pyramid was built for Pharaoh Khufu. The second, for his son Khafre, still keeps some of its smooth white outer stone at the top. The third, for Menkaure, is much smaller.

In front of them sits the Great Sphinx, a huge statue with the body of a lion and the head of a human, carved from a single ridge of rock. Smaller pyramids for queens and temples for offerings stand nearby.

How were they built?

The Egyptians had no iron tools, wheels for transport or cranes. Workers cut stone blocks, floated many of them down the Nile on boats, and dragged them up ramps on sledges — a wall painting even shows water being poured in front of a sledge to make the sand slippery.

For a long time people guessed that slaves built the pyramids. Evidence now shows they were built by paid Egyptian workers, who lived in a nearby town, ate well and were buried with honour close to the pyramids.

The pyramids today

Robbers emptied the tombs in ancient times, and the smooth outer casing stones were taken away in later centuries to build Cairo. What we see today is the stepped stone core underneath.

Even so, the pyramids still amaze visitors. They are protected as a World Heritage Site, and new scans and hidden chambers are still being studied by scientists today.

Did You Know?

The Great Pyramid is built with such care that its sides face almost exactly north, south, east and west.
