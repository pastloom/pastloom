History of Video Games

This article is written in English. Choose a language to open an automatic translation by Google Translate. Automatic translations can sometimes get historical names and dates wrong — for full accuracy, read the original English article.

Video games began as experiments on university computers — glowing dots on round screens. Within fifty years they grew into the biggest entertainment industry on Earth, bigger than films and music combined.

From Pong's two white lines to vast online worlds, this is the story of how play went electronic.

Dots, paddles and arcades

In 1962, students at MIT created Spacewar!, a duel of two tiny spaceships — a game spread from university to university by pure delight. A decade later, engineer Ralph Baer put games on home televisions with the Magnavox Odyssey, and Atari's Pong filled bars and arcades with its hypnotic blip-blip.

Arcades boomed in the late 1970s with Space Invaders and then Pac-Man, the first gaming superstar, designed to welcome everyone — its creator, Toru Iwatani, said the maze-chomping hero was inspired by a pizza with a slice missing.

Consoles, heroes and handhelds

Home consoles brought the arcade home. After a famous crash in 1983, when poor games flooded shops, Nintendo's NES (1985) restored trust with strict quality control — and Super Mario Bros. became a legend.

Nintendo's Game Boy (1989) put gaming in pockets, bundled with Tetris, the Soviet puzzle game that conquered the world. Sony's PlayStation (1994) made games cool for adults and moved them onto CDs.

Games everywhere

The internet joined players across the planet: online worlds, esports stadiums and streaming stars. In 2007 the smartphone put a game machine in every pocket, and simple games reached grandparents and toddlers alike.

Games are now used to teach, train pilots and surgeons, and tell stories as rich as novels. The glowing dots of 1962 became a universal language of play.

Did You Know?

Tetris was created in 1984 by Alexey Pajitnov in the Soviet Union — a game from behind the Iron Curtain that became one of the best-selling games of all time.
