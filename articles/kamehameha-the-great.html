Kamehameha the Great

This article is written in English. Choose a language to open an automatic translation by Google Translate. Automatic translations can sometimes get historical names and dates wrong — for full accuracy, read the original English article.

Kamehameha the Great was the chief who united the Hawaiian Islands into one kingdom. He was a giant of a man — tall, strong and famous as a warrior — but he is remembered just as much for protecting ordinary people in wartime.

Born around 1758, he rose from a young chief on the island of Hawaii to rule every island by 1810. He founded a royal line that governed Hawaii for most of the 1800s.

A chief rises

Kamehameha was born into a chiefly family and trained as a warrior. An old prophecy said a great chief would unite the islands, and many believed it meant him.

After the high chief Kalani'opu'u died in 1782, Kamehameha fought his cousin at the Battle of Moku'ohai and won control of part of the island of Hawaii. From there he built a fleet of war canoes and, with foreign guns and advisers, prepared to conquer the other islands.

Uniting the islands

In 1790 and 1791 Kamehameha took Maui and Molokai. In 1795 came his greatest victory: the Battle of Nu'uanu, where his army drove Oahu's defenders up a valley and over the cliffs of the Nu'uanu Pali.

Only Kauai and Niihau remained. Instead of more bloodshed, their chief Kaumuali'i agreed in 1810 to accept Kamehameha as king. For the first time in history, all the Hawaiian Islands had one ruler.

The law of the splintered paddle

Kamehameha is loved for a famous law. As a young warrior he had attacked some fishermen, and one broke a paddle over his head defending himself. Years later, as king, he made the Law of the Splintered Paddle: travellers and the weak must be safe on the roads, even in war.

As king he encouraged trade, farming and fishing, and kept Hawaii independent while foreign ships crowded its ports. When he died in 1819, his son Liholiho became Kamehameha II.

Did You Know?

Kamehameha was so strong that he is said to have lifted the Naha Stone, a huge rock that legend said only the uniter of Hawaii could move.
