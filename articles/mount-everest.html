Mount Everest

This article is written in English. Choose a language to open an automatic translation by Google Translate. Automatic translations can sometimes get historical names and dates wrong — for full accuracy, read the original English article.

Mount Everest is the highest mountain on Earth above sea level. It stands 8,849 metres tall in the Himalaya mountains, on the border between Nepal and Tibet.

For centuries Everest watched over the valleys below, sacred to the peoples who lived in its shadow. Only in 1953 did human beings first stand on its summit.

The roof of the world

Everest is part of the Himalaya range, raised when the land of India slowly crashed into Asia — a collision that is still pushing the mountains upward today. From the air, Everest stands among a family of giant peaks.

The mountain has long been holy to local peoples. Tibetans call it Chomolungma, 'Goddess Mother of the World', and a Buddhist monastery stands on its northern slopes, among the highest places of worship on Earth.

The race to climb it

British expeditions tried Everest through the 1920s. In 1924 George Mallory and Andrew Irvine vanished high on the mountain — whether they reached the top remains one of climbing's great mysteries.

In 1953 a British-led expedition returned. On 29 May, Edmund Hillary of New Zealand and Tenzing Norgay, a Sherpa of Nepal, climbed the final ridge and stood on the summit together — the first people known to reach the highest place on Earth.

Everest today

Since 1953, thousands of climbers have reached the summit, and famous routes have been climbed without bottled oxygen and in winter. The Sherpa people, whose strength and skill make most climbs possible, are the true heroes of the mountain.

Success has brought problems too: crowds on the narrow ridges, litter and lost equipment. Nepal and local communities now work to clean the mountain and protect the sacred roof of the world.

Did You Know?

Mount Everest grows about as fast as your fingernails — the whole Himalaya range is still being pushed upward as India presses into Asia.
