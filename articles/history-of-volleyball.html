History of Volleyball

This article is written in English. Choose a language to open an automatic translation by Google Translate. Automatic translations can sometimes get historical names and dates wrong — for full accuracy, read the original English article.

Volleyball is a game of flying leaps and thundering spikes, played by two teams separated by a high net.

It was invented in America in 1895 as a gentle game for older businessmen who found the brand-new sport of basketball too rough. It grew into one of the most played sports on Earth.

A kinder game is invented

William Morgan worked at the YMCA in Holyoke, where businessman members complained that James Naismith's new game of basketball — invented just four years earlier in a nearby town — was too strenuous for them.

Morgan blended ideas from tennis, handball and basketball: a net raised to about 6 feet 6 inches, and a light ball that players volleyed back and forth without letting it touch the ground. He called it mintonette; a watching professor suggested 'volley ball', and the name stuck.

Around the world with the YMCA

YMCA instructors and American soldiers carried volleyball across the world in the early 1900s — to the Philippines, Japan, Russia and South America, where each land added its own style.

After the Second World War the FIVB was founded in Paris (1947), the first world championships were held, and in 1964 volleyball entered the Tokyo Olympics — for both men and women, a rare equality for its time.

From gyms to beaches

In the 1900s volleyball split into two great forms: the indoor game of six players, with its soaring spikes and blocks, and beach volleyball, two against two on sand — born in California and Hawaii and an Olympic sport since 1996.

Today volleyball is among the world's most-played team sports, loved from Brazilian beaches to Japanese school gyms — a long way from a gentle game for tired businessmen.

Did You Know?

Volleyball and basketball were invented only about 15 kilometres and four years apart — two of the world's biggest sports were born in neighbouring Massachusetts towns in the 1890s.
