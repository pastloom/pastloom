Mao Zedong

This article is written in English. Choose a language to open an automatic translation by Google Translate. Automatic translations can sometimes get historical names and dates wrong — for full accuracy, read the original English article.

Mao Zedong was the Communist leader who founded the People's Republic of China in 1949 and ruled the country until his death in 1976. He led his party through a long civil war and the famous Long March to power.

Mao changed China completely — ending the old order, uniting the country and pushing through huge reforms. But some of his campaigns, above all the Great Leap Forward and the Cultural Revolution, caused terrible suffering for millions of people.

From village boy to revolutionary

Mao was born into a peasant family in Hunan province and worked his way to school in the city, where he became a librarian's assistant and read about revolution. China was in chaos: the last emperor had fallen, and warlords fought over the country.

Mao helped found the Chinese Communist Party in 1921. When the Nationalist government tried to destroy the Communists, Mao built a base in the mountains and an army of peasant soldiers.

The Long March and victory

In 1934, surrounded by Nationalist armies, the Communists broke out and began the Long March — a retreat of some 9,000 kilometres over mountains and rivers, during which most of the marchers died or were left behind. The survivors, led by Mao, reached safety in the north.

After years of war against Japan and then renewed civil war, Mao's forces won. On 1 October 1949 he stood above Tiananmen Square in Beijing and proclaimed the People's Republic of China.

China under Mao

Mao's government united China, spread schooling and basic healthcare, and gave land to peasants. But his Great Leap Forward of 1958–62, meant to industrialise China at a stroke, collapsed into a famine in which tens of millions died.

In 1966 he launched the Cultural Revolution, urging young Red Guards to attack 'old ideas' and his own party officials. Schools closed, treasures were smashed and many people were persecuted before order returned. Mao remains a giant and controversial figure: founder of modern China, and author of some of its greatest disasters.

Did You Know?

Mao's Little Red Book — Quotations from Chairman Mao — was printed in more than a billion copies during his lifetime, making it one of the most printed books in history.
