History of Coffee

This article is written in English. Choose a language to open an automatic translation by Google Translate. Automatic translations can sometimes get historical names and dates wrong — for full accuracy, read the original English article.

Coffee began in the highlands of Ethiopia, in East Africa. Legend says a goat herder named Kaldi noticed his goats dancing with energy after eating red berries from a certain bush.

From Ethiopia, coffee spread to Arabia, then to the Ottoman Empire and Europe. Coffeehouses — places to drink coffee and talk — changed cities, business and even politics.

From Ethiopia to Arabia

People in Ethiopia chewed coffee berries and mixed them with fat for energy on long journeys. Across the Red Sea in Yemen, Sufi Muslims drank coffee to stay awake during night prayers.

Yemeni farmers grew coffee on terraced hills and shipped it from the port of Mocha — a name coffee lovers still know. By the 1500s, coffeehouses had opened in Makkah, Cairo and Istanbul, where people gathered to talk, play games and hear news over cups of coffee.

Coffeehouses change the world

Coffee reached Venice and then the rest of Europe. London's coffeehouses, opening from the 1650s, were nicknamed penny universities — for a penny, anyone could drink coffee and hear news, science and debate.

Business grew in coffeehouses too. Lloyd's coffee house in London, where sailors and merchants met, grew into Lloyd's of London, the famous insurance market. Religious and political leaders sometimes tried to ban coffee, fearing it kept people awake and talking — but the drink was too popular to stop.

Coffee goes global

European powers planted coffee in their colonies — the Dutch in Java, and growers in the Caribbean and the Americas. Brazil became the world's biggest producer, and coffee became a crop that shaped whole countries.

Instant coffee, mass-produced from the early 1900s, carried coffee into ordinary homes. Today coffee is one of the most traded goods on Earth — around two billion cups are drunk every day, from tiny espresso cups in Italy to sweet iced coffee in Vietnam.

Did You Know?

Coffee was banned several times in history — in Makkah, Cairo and even Sweden — but people loved it so much that every ban failed.
