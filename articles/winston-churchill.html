Winston Churchill

This article is written in English. Choose a language to open an automatic translation by Google Translate. Automatic translations can sometimes get historical names and dates wrong — for full accuracy, read the original English article.

Winston Churchill was the British prime minister who led his country through the darkest days of the Second World War, when Britain stood almost alone against Nazi Germany.

His speeches — promising 'blood, toil, tears and sweat' and vowing 'we shall never surrender' — kept a frightened nation fighting. He is remembered as one of history's great war leaders and speakers.

A long road to the top

Churchill was a soldier, war reporter and writer before he entered politics. He escaped from a prison camp during the Boer War, served in the First World War, and held many of the top jobs in government.

In the 1930s, out of office, he warned again and again about the rise of Hitler, while most leaders hoped for peace. When war came in 1939 and Britain's early efforts failed, the country turned to the man who had been right.

Britain's finest hour

Churchill became prime minister on 10 May 1940 — the very day Germany attacked in the west. France fell within weeks, and invasion threatened Britain itself.

His answer was words as weapons. He told Parliament he had nothing to offer but 'blood, toil, tears and sweat', promised 'we shall fight on the beaches... we shall never surrender', and praised the RAF pilots of the Battle of Britain: 'Never... was so much owed by so many to so few.' Through the Blitz, his voice on the radio held the country together until the Soviet Union and the United States joined the war, and the tide slowly turned.

Victory, defeat and a return

Weeks after victory in Europe in 1945, British voters removed Churchill's party from power — to his shock. He returned as prime minister in 1951, older and unwell, and retired in 1955.

In 1946 his 'Iron Curtain' speech warned of Soviet power dividing Europe, naming the Cold War before it fully began. He died in 1965 and received a state funeral, mourned around the world.

Did You Know?

Churchill won the Nobel Prize not for peace, but for Literature — for his histories and speeches. He wrote more words than Shakespeare and Dickens combined, and his history of the Second World War runs to six large volumes.
