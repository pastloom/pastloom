The Amazon River

This article is written in English. Choose a language to open an automatic translation by Google Translate. Automatic translations can sometimes get historical names and dates wrong — for full accuracy, read the original English article.

The Amazon is the greatest river on Earth. It is about 6,400 kilometres long, and it carries more water than any other river in the world — about a fifth of all the river water that flows into the oceans.

The river begins high in the Andes mountains of Peru and crosses South America to the Atlantic Ocean. Around it grows the Amazon rainforest, the largest forest on the planet.

The mightiest river on Earth

The Amazon is fed by more than a thousand smaller rivers. In the rainy season it can be over 40 kilometres wide in places, and ocean ships can sail more than 1,500 kilometres inland to the city of Manaus.

People have lived along the Amazon for thousands of years. Long before Europeans arrived, Indigenous peoples farmed, fished and traded along its banks, and large settlements stood where the river's floods fed the soil.

Orellana and the name Amazon

In 1541 the Spanish explorer Francisco de Orellana set out from Peru looking for gold and cinnamon, and ended up sailing the whole length of the river to the Atlantic — the first European to do so.

Orellana reported battles with fierce women warriors, and named the river after the Amazons of Greek legend. His journey showed Europe the true size of South America — and began centuries of exploration, rubber booms and conflict along the river.

The river and the rainforest today

The Amazon rainforest is sometimes called 'the lungs of the Earth' because its trees take in so much carbon and give out so much oxygen. The river and forest together hold an unmatched share of the world's plants and animals.

Today the Amazon faces cutting of its forests, mining and changing rains. Protecting the river means protecting the forest, the peoples who live in it, and the climate of the whole planet.

Did You Know?

So much fresh water pours from the Amazon that the Atlantic Ocean around its mouth is noticeably less salty for many kilometres out to sea.
