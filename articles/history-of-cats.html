History of Cats

This article is written in English. Choose a language to open an automatic translation by Google Translate. Automatic translations can sometimes get historical names and dates wrong — for full accuracy, read the original English article.

Cats are among the most loved pets in the world, but for most of history they were working animals, not pets. People first welcomed cats because cats hunted the mice and rats that ate stored grain.

Over about 10,000 years, the wild cat slowly became the house cat. Cats travelled on ships, lived in temples and palaces, and today they live in homes on every continent.

From wildcat to farm cat

When people began farming, they stored grain in villages — and grain attracted mice. Wildcats came to hunt the mice, and the boldest, calmest cats stayed close to people.

A cat buried beside a human on the island of Cyprus about 9,500 years ago shows how early this friendship began. Cats were never fully tamed like dogs; they chose us as much as we chose them.

Cats in Egypt and beyond

In ancient Egypt, cats were honoured. The goddess Bastet was shown with a cat's head, hurting a cat could be punished, and beloved cats were mummified like royalty.

Sailors and traders carried cats across the world on ships, where cats protected food stores from rats. In this way cats spread to Europe and Asia, guarding homes, temples and silk stores.

Loved, feared, and loved again

In medieval Europe, feelings about cats were mixed, and old superstitions linked black cats with bad luck. In much of the Islamic world, by contrast, cats were respected as clean animals and welcomed in homes and mosques.

In the 1800s the first cat shows were held in London, and keeping cats as companions became fashionable. Today the once-wild hunter sleeps on sofas in hundreds of millions of homes.

Did You Know?

Sailors believed a ship's cat brought good luck — and a cat falling overboard was said to mean a storm was coming.
