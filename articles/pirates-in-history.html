Pirates in History

This article is written in English. Choose a language to open an automatic translation by Google Translate. Automatic translations can sometimes get historical names and dates wrong — for full accuracy, read the original English article.

Pirates are robbers who attack ships at sea. They have existed since ancient times, wherever trade ships carried valuable goods.

The most famous pirates sailed in the Caribbean about three hundred years ago, during what is called the Golden Age of Piracy. Their real lives were rougher and shorter than the pirate stories we love today.

Pirates of the ancient and medieval seas

Piracy began as soon as rich cargoes took to the sea. In 75 BCE, the young Julius Caesar was captured by pirates and held for ransom — he joked with them, then returned after his release and had them executed.

In later centuries, Viking raiders, Barbary corsairs on the North African coast and pirates in Asian seas all preyed on merchant ships.

The Golden Age of Piracy

Between about 1650 and 1730, thousands of pirates roamed the Caribbean and beyond. Many were sailors left jobless when wars ended, turning to robbery at sea.

Blackbeard frightened his enemies by weaving smoking fuses into his great black beard. Anne Bonny and Mary Read became famous as women pirates. Pirate crews often shared their loot fairly and elected their captains — but their world was violent, and most pirates died young.

The end of the golden age

Governments finally sent powerful navies to hunt the pirates down. Captured pirates were often hanged in public at the harbour, as a warning to others.

By about 1730 the Golden Age was over. Pirates still exist in a few seas today, but the sailing-ship pirates live on in stories, films and the famous skull-and-crossbones flag, the Jolly Roger.

Did You Know?

Pirates really did sometimes bury treasure — but only one famous case is well documented: Captain Kidd hid loot on Gardiners Island, and the authorities dug it up to use as evidence against him.
