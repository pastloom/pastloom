History of Istanbul

This article is written in English. Choose a language to open an automatic translation by Google Translate. Automatic translations can sometimes get historical names and dates wrong — for full accuracy, read the original English article.

Istanbul is one of the few cities in the world that stands on two continents — Europe and Asia — divided by the water of the Bosphorus. It has been the capital of two great empires: the Byzantine Empire and the Ottoman Empire.

The city has had three famous names. It began as Byzantium, became Constantinople for more than a thousand years, and is known today as Istanbul, the largest city of Turkey.

Byzantium and Constantinople

Greek settlers founded Byzantium about 657 BCE on a hill where Europe faces Asia — a perfect place for trade and for defence. In 330 CE the Roman emperor Constantine rebuilt it as his new capital, Constantinople, sometimes called the New Rome.

For more than 1,000 years it was the rich, strong capital of the Byzantine Empire. Its mighty walls turned back army after army, and its great church, Hagia Sophia, built in 537 CE, was the largest cathedral in the world for nearly a thousand years.

The Ottoman capital

In 1453, after a famous siege, the Ottoman Sultan Mehmed II captured the city. He made it the capital of the Ottoman Empire, rebuilt it, and filled it with mosques, markets and palaces such as Topkapi Palace.

Under Suleiman the Magnificent, the architect Sinan crowned the hills with graceful mosques. Istanbul became a world city of trade, where ships, caravans and merchants from three continents met.

Istanbul today

After the First World War, the Ottoman Empire ended. In 1923 the new Republic of Turkey moved its capital to Ankara, and in 1930 the city was officially renamed Istanbul — but it remained Turkey's biggest and busiest city.

Today huge bridges cross the Bosphorus, joining the European and Asian sides. Minarets, churches, palaces and modern towers share its skyline, and layers of three empires can still be seen in its streets.

Did You Know?

Istanbul's Grand Bazaar, built in the 1400s, has more than 60 streets and thousands of shops — it is one of the oldest and largest covered markets in the world.
