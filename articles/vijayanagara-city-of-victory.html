Vijayanagara: The City of Victory

This article is written in English. Choose a language to open an automatic translation by Google Translate. Automatic translations can sometimes get historical names and dates wrong — for full accuracy, read the original English article.

Vijayanagara means 'City of Victory'. It was the capital of a great South Indian empire that stood from 1336 to 1646, and for two hundred years it was one of the largest and richest cities on Earth.

Foreign visitors wrote in wonder about its markets full of gems, its grand festivals and its stone temples. The city fell suddenly in 1565 — and its ruins at Hampi still amaze travellers today.

A city rises on the river

Two brothers, Harihara and Bukka, founded Vijayanagara in 1336 on the rocky banks of the Tungabhadra River. The strange landscape of giant boulders made a natural fortress.

The empire grew to rule most of South India. Its kings built tanks and canals, welcomed traders from Portugal and Persia, and protected the region's temples and learning.

The days of Krishnadevaraya

Under Krishnadevaraya the empire reached its height. He was a warrior-king who also wrote poetry and filled his court with poets of many languages.

The Portuguese visitor Domingo Paes described a city as large as Rome, with markets selling rubies, diamonds and pearls, and streets so full of goods that travellers could hardly pass. The king's great festival, Mahanavami, drew ambassadors from across Asia.

Talikota and the silent stones

In 1565 the allied Deccan sultanates defeated Vijayanagara at the Battle of Talikota. The victorious armies looted and burned the great city, and its people fled.

The empire limped on from other capitals until 1646, but the City of Victory never rose again. Today its ruins at Hampi — temples, elephant stables, a stone chariot and miles of carved walls — are a World Heritage Site.

Did You Know?

The Vittala Temple at Hampi has musical pillars — stone columns that ring with different notes when gently tapped.
