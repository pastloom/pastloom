History of Riddles

This article is written in English. Choose a language to open an automatic translation by Google Translate. Automatic translations can sometimes get historical names and dates wrong — for full accuracy, read the original English article.

A riddle is a question with a trick in it — a puzzle in words that hides its answer. Riddles are among the oldest games in literature: the earliest written ones come from Babylon, almost 4,000 years ago.

In old stories, riddles were deadly serious. The Sphinx of Egypt killed travellers who failed her riddle, and heroes like Oedipus and Samson won fame through riddles.

Riddles of life and death

In Greek myth, the Sphinx sat outside Thebes asking her famous riddle and devouring those who failed, until Oedipus answered 'Man' — who crawls as a baby, walks upright as an adult, and leans on a stick in old age.

Such stories appear worldwide: Odin wagers his head in a riddle contest with a giant, and in the Indian Mahabharata, the hero Yudhishthira saves his brothers' lives by answering the questions of a nature spirit. Riddles tested whether a stranger was wise — or edible.

Riddles in books and courts

Babylonian schoolboys copied riddles as exercises. In Anglo-Saxon England, monks wrote elegant riddles in the Exeter Book, where onions, books and storms speak in the first person and dare you to name them.

Riddle games entertained courts from Persia to Scandinavia. Losing could mean shame — or, in the old tales, your head. Even real kings collected riddle books as treasures of wit.

Riddles today

Children still trade riddles in playgrounds exactly as Babylonian schoolboys did, and 'What has keys but can't open a lock?' (a piano) would be understood in any century.

Riddles survive inside crosswords, escape rooms, video games and jokes — and inside famous books, from Alice's tea party to Gollum's cave. The oldest word game in the world is still being played.

Did You Know?

The Exeter Book's riddles have no answers written down — scholars still argue about some of them after 1,000 years, and a few may never be solved for certain.
