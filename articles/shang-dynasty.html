The Shang Dynasty

This article is written in English. Choose a language to open an automatic translation by Google Translate. Automatic translations can sometimes get historical names and dates wrong — for full accuracy, read the original English article.

The Shang dynasty, ruling from about 1600 to 1046 BCE, is the first Chinese dynasty that we can prove from archaeology. Earlier rulers may be legend, but the Shang left real writing behind.

Shang kings carved questions for their ancestors on bones and turtle shells — oracle bones. Those scratches are the oldest Chinese writing, and the direct ancestors of the characters used today.

Kings, bronze and ancestors

The Shang ruled the Yellow River valley from a series of capitals. Their kings were also chief priests, honouring royal ancestors and the high god Di with sacrifices and feasts.

Shang bronze-workers cast enormous ritual vessels of a quality the world had rarely seen. Armies fought from horse-drawn chariots, and farmers grew millet and raised silkworms.

The oracle bones

To decide about war, harvests, illness or the weather, Shang kings used oracle bones. A question was carved on an ox bone or turtle shell, heat was applied until it cracked, and the king read the answer in the cracks.

Tens of thousands of these bones have been dug up near Anyang. Their characters can still be read — proof that Chinese writing has an unbroken history of more than 3,000 years.

The fall of the Shang

The last Shang king, Di Xin, was remembered as a cruel tyrant who wasted the kingdom's strength. In about 1046 BCE the Zhou people rose against him.

At the Battle of Muye the Shang army was defeated, and the Zhou took power — explaining their victory with a new idea, the Mandate of Heaven: a bad king loses Heaven's right to rule.

Did You Know?

Oracle bone writing was rediscovered by accident in 1899, when a scholar noticed ancient characters on 'dragon bones' being sold as medicine in a Beijing pharmacy.
