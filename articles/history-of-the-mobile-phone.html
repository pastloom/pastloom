History of the Mobile Phone

This article is written in English. Choose a language to open an automatic translation by Google Translate. Automatic translations can sometimes get historical names and dates wrong — for full accuracy, read the original English article.

The mobile phone put a telephone in every pocket — and then became a camera, a map, a bank and a library too. Yet the first handheld mobile, in 1973, was the size of a brick and cost as much as a car.

In just fifty years, this one invention changed how the whole world talks, works, shops and plays more than almost any other.

The brick that started it

Car phones existed from the 1940s, but they lived in cars and served only a few callers per city. On 3 April 1973, Martin Cooper of Motorola stood on a New York street and made the first call on a truly handheld phone — to his rival at Bell Labs, to tell him the news.

The phone he used, the DynaTAC, reached shops in 1983. It weighed about a kilogram, offered half an hour of talking, and only the rich could afford it. Mobile networks — 'cellular' systems of small radio zones, or cells — slowly spread through the 1980s.

Smaller, cheaper, smarter

In the 1990s, 2G networks brought clear calls and the text message — the SMS, loved first by teenagers, which became a worldwide habit. Phones shrank, batteries lasted days, and names like Nokia ruled the market.

Cameras, music and colour screens crept in. Then in 2007 Apple launched the iPhone: a full touch screen with no keyboard, run by apps. Within a few years, nearly every phone copied the design, and the 'smartphone' age had begun.

The world in a pocket

Today mobile phones outnumber people. They carry banking to villages without banks, maps to every traveller, and news to every pocket. For billions, the phone is their first — and only — computer and camera.

The change is not finished: networks grow faster, screens fold, and satellites begin to reach phones directly. The brick of 1973 became the doorway through which most of humanity now meets the world.

Did You Know?

The first mobile phone call in 1973 was a boast: Martin Cooper called his chief rival to say he was speaking from a real handheld mobile phone — something the rival's company had failed to build first.
