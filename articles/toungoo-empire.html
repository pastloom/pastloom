The Toungoo Empire

This article is written in English. Choose a language to open an automatic translation by Google Translate. Automatic translations can sometimes get historical names and dates wrong — for full accuracy, read the original English article.

The Toungoo Empire was the largest empire in the history of mainland South-East Asia. Built by Burmese kings of the Toungoo Dynasty in the 1500s, it stretched at its height from parts of India to the borders of Vietnam.

Its greatest king, Bayinnaung, is remembered as one of the greatest conquerors in Asian history. But the empire was so large that it broke apart soon after his death, and the dynasty finally ended in 1752.

Tabinshwehti reunites Burma

After the Pagan Kingdom fell, Burma split into warring states for 250 years. From the small hill town of Toungoo, the young king Tabinshwehti began conquering in the 1530s.

With help from Portuguese mercenaries and their guns, Tabinshwehti took the rich port of Pegu and reunited most of Burma by 1550 — the first unification since Pagan.

Bayinnaung, the great conqueror

Tabinshwehti's brother-in-law and successor, Bayinnaung, campaigned for 30 years. He conquered the Shan states, Chiang Mai, Laos and — in 1569 — the great Thai kingdom of Ayutthaya.

His empire was the largest ever seen in mainland South-East Asia. Bayinnaung was also a devout Buddhist who built pagodas, banned animal sacrifice, and sent gifts to temples as far away as Sri Lanka.

Too big to hold

After Bayinnaung died in 1581, his vast empire cracked. Ayutthaya's hero-king Naresuan threw off Burmese rule and invaded Burma itself, and by 1599 the empire had collapsed into civil war.

A restored Toungoo line ruled a smaller Burma from Ava for another 150 years, until Mon rebels captured the last king in 1752 — ending the dynasty.

Did You Know?

Bayinnaung kept a famous white elephant and once went to war partly over white elephants — rare albino elephants were believed to bring a king good fortune.
