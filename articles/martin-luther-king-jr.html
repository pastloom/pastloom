Martin Luther King Jr.

This article is written in English. Choose a language to open an automatic translation by Google Translate. Automatic translations can sometimes get historical names and dates wrong — for full accuracy, read the original English article.

Martin Luther King Jr. was an American Baptist minister who led the fight for equal rights for Black Americans in the 1950s and 1960s.

He believed in peaceful protest, not violence. His famous I Have a Dream speech is still one of the most loved speeches in the world.

Segregation in America

When King was young, the southern United States had laws that separated Black and white people in schools, buses, restaurants and even drinking fountains.

In 1955, after Rosa Parks was arrested for refusing to give up her bus seat, King helped lead a bus boycott in Montgomery that lasted over a year — and won.

Marches and a dream

King led peaceful marches and gave speeches calling for jobs, voting rights and equal treatment. He was arrested many times, but he refused to answer hate with hate.

On 28 August 1963, before a huge crowd in Washington, he gave his I Have a Dream speech, describing a future where people are judged by their character, not by the colour of their skin.

His death and his legacy

New civil rights laws in 1964 and 1965 ended legal segregation and protected voting rights — victories his movement helped to win.

King was shot and killed in 1968, at only 39 years old. The United States now honours him with a national holiday on the third Monday of January each year.

Did You Know?

At 35, Martin Luther King Jr. was the youngest person of his time to receive the Nobel Peace Prize.
