History of Mumbai

This article is written in English. Choose a language to open an automatic translation by Google Translate. Automatic translations can sometimes get historical names and dates wrong — for full accuracy, read the original English article.

Mumbai, on India's west coast, is the country's city of business and film — home of Bollywood and of India's busiest dreams. It began as seven separate islands, fished by the Koli people for centuries.

Through land reclamation the seven islands were joined into one, and the small Portuguese and British trading post of Bombay grew into Mumbai, a metropolis of more than 20 million people.

Seven islands

The Koli fishing community lived on the islands of Bombay long before written records, and the goddess Mumbadevi, whom they honoured, still gives the city its modern name. The islands were ruled in turn by Hindu kings, the sultans of Gujarat, and in 1534 by the Portuguese.

In 1661 the islands passed to King Charles II of England as part of the dowry of the Portuguese princess Catherine of Braganza. He rented them to the East India Company, which saw the deep, safe harbour and began building a trading city.

Bombay grows rich

Workers filled the sea channels between the islands with earth, joining them into one long island — the great Hornby Vellard causeway of 1784 was an early triumph. Forts, docks and mills rose, and Bombay grew rich on cotton, especially when the American Civil War cut off supplies to Britain in the 1860s.

In 1853 India's first railway ran from Bombay to Thane, and grand Gothic buildings — the Victoria Terminus station, the university, the high court — gave the city its imperial face. Freedom fighters also made Bombay a centre of India's independence movement; Gandhi launched the Quit India movement here in 1942.

Mumbai today

After independence in 1947, Bombay became the capital of Maharashtra state in 1960, and in 1995 it was officially renamed Mumbai. Its film industry, Bollywood, makes more films than any other city in the world.

Today Mumbai is India's financial heart — its stock exchanges, banks and film studios draw people from every corner of India chasing opportunity. The old seven islands are now one crowded, energetic peninsula, still washed by the Arabian Sea.

Did You Know?

India's very first train journey in 1853 ran from Bombay to Thane — about 34 kilometres — and crowds lined the track to watch the steam engine pass.
