The Roman Republic

This article is written in English. Choose a language to open an automatic translation by Google Translate. Automatic translations can sometimes get historical names and dates wrong — for full accuracy, read the original English article.

The Roman Republic was the system of government that ruled Rome for nearly 500 years, from 509 to 27 BCE. Instead of a king, Rome was run by elected officials and a powerful council called the Senate.

Under the Republic, Rome grew from one city in Italy to the master of the Mediterranean world — before civil wars tore the system apart and it gave way to emperors.

After the kings

In 509 BCE the Romans drove out their last king, Tarquin the Proud, and swore never to be ruled by one man again. Power was shared so no single person could seize it.

Two consuls held the top job together for just one year. A senate of experienced elders advised them, and assemblies of citizens voted on laws and elections.

The struggle of the orders

At first only rich patrician families could hold office. Ordinary citizens — the plebeians — fought for centuries to win rights: their own representatives called tribunes, written laws, and finally a share of the consulship itself.

This long, mostly peaceful struggle made the Republic flexible and strong — Rome could change without breaking.

Conquest, civil wars and the fall

Republican armies conquered Italy, then defeated the mighty sea-power Carthage in the Punic Wars, then took Greece and the lands of Alexander's successors.

Victory brought wealth, slaves and vast farms — but also poverty for small farmers, huge inequality, and generals whose soldiers were loyal to them, not to Rome.

Reformers like the Gracchi brothers were murdered by their opponents. Rival generals — Marius, Sulla, Pompey and Julius Caesar — marched armies against Rome itself.

After Caesar's assassination, his heir Octavian defeated all rivals and in 27 BCE took the name Augustus. The Republic was over, though Romans kept its forms and titles for centuries.

Did You Know?

The letters SPQR, seen on Roman banners and still on drains in Rome today, stand for Senatus Populusque Romanus — the Senate and People of Rome, the twin powers of the Republic.
