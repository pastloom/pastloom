Mumtaz Mahal

This article is written in English. Choose a language to open an automatic translation by Google Translate. Automatic translations can sometimes get historical names and dates wrong — for full accuracy, read the original English article.

Mumtaz Mahal was the beloved wife of the Mughal emperor Shah Jahan. When she died in 1631, the heartbroken emperor built the Taj Mahal in Agra as her tomb — one of the most beautiful buildings in the world.

In life, Mumtaz was more than a queen in a love story. She was Shah Jahan's closest adviser, travelled with him across the empire, and her seal appeared on royal orders.

A marriage of love and trust

Arjumand Banu was the daughter of Asaf Khan, a powerful Mughal noble, and niece of Empress Nur Jahan. She married Prince Khurram — the future Shah Jahan — in 1612, after a five-year engagement.

When Khurram became emperor in 1628, he gave her the title Mumtaz Mahal, meaning Jewel of the Palace. She kept the royal seal, advised him on petitions, and was known for her charity to widows and the poor.

Death at Burhanpur

Mumtaz travelled with Shah Jahan even on military campaigns. In 1631, while with him at Burhanpur in central India, she died shortly after giving birth to their 14th child, a daughter.

Stories say the emperor's hair turned grey with grief. Her body was later carried to Agra, to a garden plot beside the Yamuna River that she herself had loved.

The Taj Mahal

Shah Jahan ordered a tomb beyond compare. Work on the Taj Mahal began in 1632 and took more than 20 years, with thousands of craftsmen inlaying white marble with flowers of precious stone.

When Shah Jahan died in 1666, he was buried beside Mumtaz under the great dome. The Taj Mahal is now a UNESCO World Heritage Site and a Wonder of the World — and it began as one man's memorial to her.

Did You Know?

Mumtaz Mahal's great-aunt was Nur Jahan, the powerful empress — making her part of two generations of remarkable Mughal women.
