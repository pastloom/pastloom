History of Camping

This article is written in English. Choose a language to open an automatic translation by Google Translate. Automatic translations can sometimes get historical names and dates wrong — for full accuracy, read the original English article.

Camping means sleeping outdoors for pleasure — in a tent, under the stars, or in a caravan — usually in the countryside. Soldiers, shepherds and travellers camped from necessity for thousands of years, but camping as a holiday only began in the late 1800s.

Its founder was Thomas Hiram Holding, an English tailor who wrote the first camper's handbook and started the world's first camping club.

From survival to pleasure

Armies, explorers and nomads always camped — but because they had to. The strange new idea of the 1800s was camping because you wanted to: sleeping in a tent as a rest from city life, factories and smoke.

Thomas Hiram Holding had crossed the American prairies as a boy and canoed and cycled through Ireland and Scotland as a man. He believed ordinary people could enjoy the wild cheaply, with a light tent and a bicycle.

Clubs, scouts and handbooks

In 1901 Holding founded the Cycle and Camp Club, the first camping club, and in 1908 he published The Camper's Handbook, teaching everything from pitching a tent to cooking over a fire.

At the same time, Robert Baden-Powell held his first Boy Scout camp on Brownsea Island in 1907. Scouting carried camping to millions of children, and the campfire, the tent and the backpack became symbols of healthy youth.

Cars, caravans and glamping

In the 1920s and 1930s, cheap cars changed camping: families could drive to the coast or mountains with heavy canvas tents, folding beds and stoves. Campsites with water and toilets appeared, and caravans — homes on wheels — followed.

After the Second World War, camping became the classic affordable holiday. Today it stretches from a lone hiker's tiny tent to luxury 'glamping' sites — but the pleasure Holding promised is unchanged: waking up outdoors.

Did You Know?

Thomas Hiram Holding designed his own lightweight tent and camping gear because shops sold nothing suitable — early campers had to sew, build or adapt almost everything they used.
