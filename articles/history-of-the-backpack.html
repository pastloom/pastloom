History of the Backpack

This article is written in English. Choose a language to open an automatic translation by Google Translate. Automatic translations can sometimes get historical names and dates wrong — for full accuracy, read the original English article.

A backpack carries your load on both shoulders, leaving your hands free. People have carried loads on their backs for thousands of years — Ötzi the Iceman, found frozen in the Alps, may have carried a simple frame pack more than 5,000 years ago.

The modern backpack was shaped by soldiers, mountain climbers and schoolchildren — in that order.

Carrying on the back

Back-carrying is one of humanity's oldest technologies. Farmers, porters and traders across the world carried baskets and bundles on wooden frames, sometimes with a strap across the forehead, so the legs and back did the work.

When Ötzi the Iceman's body was found in the Alps in 1991, the wooden frame and hide found with him led archaeologists to think he carried a simple backpack — over 5,300 years ago.

Soldiers and climbers

In the 1800s, armies standardised the knapsack — a stiff box of leather or canvas holding a soldier's whole kit. German soldiers carried the softer 'rucksack', whose name English borrowed. Two world wars made the military pack tougher and more practical.

Mountain climbers demanded more. In the 1950s, makers like Dick Kelty added aluminium frames and — a true revolution — padded hip belts that moved the load from the shoulders to the hips. In the 1970s, internal-frame packs hugged the body for climbing and skiing.

The backpack everywhere

New nylon fabrics made packs light, strong and colourful. In the 1970s and 1980s, students abandoned briefcases and book straps for backpacks, and companies founded for climbers found themselves supplying every school in the land.

Today the backpack carries laptops to offices, lunches to building sites and tents across continents. The soldier's knapsack has become the most universal bag on Earth.

Did You Know?

Ötzi the Iceman, whose body was found in the Alps in 1991, died about 5,300 years ago — and archaeologists think the wooden frame found with him was part of a backpack.
