The House of Wisdom in Baghdad

This article is written in English. Choose a language to open an automatic translation by Google Translate. Automatic translations can sometimes get historical names and dates wrong — for full accuracy, read the original English article.

The House of Wisdom — Bayt al-Hikma in Arabic — was a great library and translation centre in Baghdad, the Abbasid capital, about 1,200 years ago.

There, scholars translated the learning of Greece, Persia and India into Arabic, and then went further, adding discoveries of their own. It became a symbol of the Islamic Golden Age of science.

Baghdad calls for books

The Abbasid caliphs wanted the knowledge of the whole world in their capital. Caliph al-Ma'mun, who reigned from 813 to 833, gave strong support to the House of Wisdom and its translators.

Envoys brought manuscripts from Byzantine lands, Persia and India. A famous team led by the translator Hunayn ibn Ishaq turned Greek works by Aristotle, Galen and Euclid into Arabic — and was said to be paid the weight of its books in gold.

More than translation

The scholars of Baghdad did not only copy old books. They checked them, corrected them and built on them — in algebra, astronomy, optics, geography and medicine.

The mathematician al-Khwarizmi worked in Baghdad in these years, and the astronomers of the city measured the Earth and mapped the stars with instruments better than any before them.

What became of it

As Abbasid power weakened, the great age of the House of Wisdom passed, though learning continued in Baghdad and spread to Cairo, Córdoba and Persia.

In 1258 the Mongols destroyed Baghdad. Later storytellers claimed the River Tigris ran black with ink from thrown-away books — an image of how much learning the city had held. The translations survived elsewhere, and centuries later many reached Europe, helping to spark its own scientific rebirth.

Did You Know?

Baghdad's translators were sometimes paid, so the story goes, the weight of each finished book in gold — knowledge was quite literally worth its weight in gold.
