History of Novels

This article is written in English. Choose a language to open an automatic translation by Google Translate. Automatic translations can sometimes get historical names and dates wrong — for full accuracy, read the original English article.

A novel is a long made-up story written in prose. Novels let readers live other lives — sail with pirates, solve crimes, fall in love, or travel to worlds that never existed.

The book often called the world's first novel was written in Japan more than 1,000 years ago by a lady of the royal court. Since then, the novel has become the most popular form of literature on Earth.

The first novels

Around the year 1008, Murasaki Shikibu wrote The Tale of Genji, the long story of a prince and the court of Japan. Its deep look into feelings and daily life makes many readers call it the first true novel.

Long stories in prose appeared in other lands too. In Spain in 1605, Cervantes published Don Quixote, about a foolish knight who tilts at windmills — funny, sad, and the model for the modern European novel.

The novel's golden age

In the 1700s and 1800s, printing grew cheaper and readers multiplied. Novels were published in magazines piece by piece, and families waited eagerly for the next part of a Dickens story.

Jane Austen wrote of love and manners in English villages, Tolstoy of war and peace in Russia, and writers across Asia, Africa and the Americas began telling their own worlds in novels. For the first time, ordinary people's lives were the centre of great books.

Novels today

In the 1900s, cheap paperbacks put novels in every pocket, and bookshops and libraries spread worldwide. New kinds of novels appeared: detective stories, science fiction, fantasy and tales for children and young readers.

Today novels are also read on phones and listened to as audiobooks, and prizes and films carry them across languages. The long story Murasaki began is still the world's favourite way to read.

Did You Know?

Charles Dickens published most of his novels in monthly parts — readers queued at ports in America to grab the latest instalment arriving by ship and learn what happened next.
