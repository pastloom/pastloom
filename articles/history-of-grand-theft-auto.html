The History of Grand Theft Auto

This article is written in English. Choose a language to open an automatic translation by Google Translate. Automatic translations can sometimes get historical names and dates wrong — for full accuracy, read the original English article.

Grand Theft Auto — GTA for short — is one of the best-selling game series in history. Take-Two has reported more than 480 million copies sold across the series, from a small top-down game made in Dundee, Scotland in 1997 to the giant Grand Theft Auto VI in 2026.

Its biggest leap came in 2001, when GTA III put players inside a living 3D city for the first time. Almost every open-world game since has followed the path GTA cut.

A Scottish experiment: cops become robbers

The series was born at DMA Design, a small studio in Dundee, Scotland, already known for the gentle puzzle game Lemmings. Its new project, begun in 1995, was called Race 'n' Chase — and at first, the player was a police officer chasing criminals.

Testing showed that playing the criminal was far more fun. The game changed sides, took the name Grand Theft Auto, and launched on 28 November 1997 with a view from above of three cities: Liberty City, San Andreas and Vice City. Politicians in Britain argued about it in Parliament — and the arguments only made it sell more. A sequel, GTA 2, followed in 1999.

The 3D revolution

In December 1998, brothers Sam and Dan Houser helped found Rockstar Games in New York, and DMA Design later became Rockstar North. Their great gamble came in 2001: Grand Theft Auto III rebuilt the series in full 3D. Players could walk, drive and explore a whole city — Liberty City — in almost any order they wished. It became the best-selling game of 2001 in America and set the standard for open-world games.

Rockstar followed at speed. Vice City (2002) glowed with 1980s neon and pop music. San Andreas (2004) offered a whole state to roam, with three cities, countryside and deserts. GTA IV (2008) returned to Liberty City with a sadder, more serious story, following immigrant Niko Bellic's search for the American dream.

GTA V, GTA Online and the road to VI

Grand Theft Auto V arrived in September 2013, set in Los Santos, Rockstar's version of Los Angeles, with three playable heroes planning great robberies together. It was reported to have made $1 billion in just three days — faster than any entertainment product before it — and GTA Online, launched weeks later, kept it alive for thirteen years.

By the time Grand Theft Auto VI launched on 19 November 2026, the series had travelled from tiny cars on a Scottish screen to the biggest entertainment launch in history. Eight main games across twenty-nine years had made three fictional cities — Liberty City, Vice City and Los Santos — into places known all over the world.

Did You Know?

The first GTA was nearly cancelled more than once — early versions were buggy and dull. Only when the makers let players play as the criminal, free to roam anywhere, did the game suddenly come alive.
