Hagia Sophia

This article is written in English. Choose a language to open an automatic translation by Google Translate. Automatic translations can sometimes get historical names and dates wrong — for full accuracy, read the original English article.

Hagia Sophia, in Istanbul, Turkey, is a building with a giant dome that seems to float above the floor. The Byzantine emperor Justinian built it as a church in just six years, finishing in 537.

For almost a thousand years it was the largest cathedral in the world. It later became a mosque, then a museum, and is a mosque again today.

Justinian's wonder

Two earlier churches on the site had burned down in riots. In 532 the emperor Justinian ordered a church unlike anything before it, designed by two mathematician-engineers, Anthemius and Isidore.

Thousands of workers finished it in under six years — astonishing for its size. When Justinian entered and saw the great dome, legend says he cried: Solomon, I have outdone thee!

The floating dome

The dome rests on a ring of forty windows, so light pours in around its base and it seems to hover in the air. Below it, the vast open space amazed everyone who entered — architects studied it for centuries, and its design inspired domes across the world.

Earthquakes cracked the dome and part of it fell in 558; it was rebuilt higher and stronger, and great buttresses were added over the centuries to hold the building firm.

Church, mosque, museum, mosque

For over 900 years Hagia Sophia was the heart of Orthodox Christianity. In 1453 the Ottoman sultan Mehmed II conquered Constantinople and made it a mosque, adding minarets; its Christian mosaics were covered with plaster, which accidentally preserved them.

In 1935 Turkey's leader Atatürk turned it into a museum, and the hidden mosaics were uncovered again. In 2020 it became a working mosque once more, while remaining open to visitors — a single building carrying fifteen centuries of history.

Did You Know?

Hagia Sophia was built so fast and so boldly that its dome partially collapsed in an earthquake just 21 years after it opened — and was rebuilt even higher.
