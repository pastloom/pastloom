The Song Dynasty: China's Age of Invention

This article is written in English. Choose a language to open an automatic translation by Google Translate. Automatic translations can sometimes get historical names and dates wrong — for full accuracy, read the original English article.

The Song dynasty ruled China from 960 to 1279. Its armies were often weak, threatened by strong neighbours — but its cities, trade and inventions were the most advanced in the world.

In Song times, China used printed books, paper money, gunpowder weapons and the compass at sea. Its capital cities were bursting with restaurants, theatres, printed adverts and busy canals.

Cities like the modern world

Song cities were astonishing for their time. Kaifeng and later Hangzhou had multi-storey restaurants, tea houses, markets open at night, and food delivery for the rich.

China's population passed 100 million, fed by new fast-growing rice from Vietnam. Iron works, shipyards and silk workshops made Song China the richest economy on Earth.

An age of invention

The Song state printed books on a huge scale, so learning spread far beyond the palace. Around 1040 the craftsman Bi Sheng invented movable type, centuries before it was used in Europe.

Song sailors navigated with the magnetic compass, merchants paid with the world's first government paper money, and Song armies fired early gunpowder bombs and rockets against invaders.

Falling to the Mongols

The Song never controlled all of old China. First the Jin people took the north, forcing the court south to Hangzhou in 1127; then the Mongols came.

After decades of resistance, the last Song emperor, a child, was lost at sea in the final battle of 1279. China was united again — but under Mongol rule.

Did You Know?

Song dynasty Hangzhou so amazed Marco Polo, who saw it under Mongol rule, that he called it the finest and most splendid city in the world.
