History of the Toothbrush

This article is written in English. Choose a language to open an automatic translation by Google Translate. Automatic translations can sometimes get historical names and dates wrong — for full accuracy, read the original English article.

People have cleaned their teeth for thousands of years. Long before the toothbrush, people in Babylon and Egypt chewed special sticks until one end frayed into a soft brush, and used it to scrub their teeth clean.

The modern toothbrush — a handle with bristles — began in China about 500 years ago, travelled slowly to Europe, and became an everyday object for everyone only in the last hundred years.

Sticks, twigs and toothpicks

The oldest tooth-cleaning tools were chew sticks. People in Babylon and Egypt chewed the end of a twig until it split into fibres, then rubbed their teeth with the soft, brush-like end.

In the Middle East and Asia, the favourite twig was the miswak, cut from the Salvadora persica tree, whose wood naturally freshens the mouth. The Greeks and Romans used toothpicks, rough cloths and powders made of crushed shells and bones.

The bristle brush from China

By about 1498, brush-makers in China were fixing stiff hairs from the necks of Siberian hogs into handles of bamboo or bone — the first true toothbrushes. Travellers carried the idea to Europe in the 1600s.

In Europe the brushes were expensive luxuries, and many people still cleaned their teeth with a cloth and soot or salt. In England in 1780, William Addis began making toothbrushes in large numbers — the story says he drilled holes in a bone and tied in bristles while he was in prison, then built a business on the idea.

A brush for everyone

In 1857 the toothbrush received its first patent in the United States, but most people still did not brush every day. That changed in 1938, when the company DuPont put soft nylon bristles in place of animal hair — cheaper, cleaner and gentler.

Soldiers in the Second World War were required to brush daily, and they brought the habit home. The first electric toothbrush followed in Switzerland in 1954, and today the simple brush — now often made of bamboo — is one of the most common objects on Earth.

Did You Know?

A prisoner is said to have invented the mass-produced toothbrush: William Addis made his first brush from a bone and bristles while in prison in England in 1780 — then built a business that sold brushes around the world.
