William the Conqueror

This article is written in English. Choose a language to open an automatic translation by Google Translate. Automatic translations can sometimes get historical names and dates wrong — for full accuracy, read the original English article.

William the Conqueror was the Duke of Normandy who invaded England in 1066, defeated King Harold at the Battle of Hastings, and became king himself. His victory is called the Norman Conquest.

William changed England forever. He built castles across the land, brought the French language to its court, and ordered the Domesday Book — the most complete survey of a kingdom made anywhere in medieval Europe.

Duke of Normandy

William was the son of a Norman duke, born in Falaise in northern France. The Normans were descendants of Vikings who had settled there and learned to speak French.

He became duke as a boy of about eight and survived a dangerous childhood of plots and rebellions. By adulthood he was a tough, determined ruler — and he believed the childless King Edward of England had promised him the English throne.

1066: the Battle of Hastings

When Edward died in 1066, the English chose Harold Godwinson as king instead. William gathered ships and soldiers, crossed the Channel, and met Harold's army near Hastings on 14 October 1066.

It was a long, hard battle. Harold was killed — legend says by an arrow in the eye — and William marched to London, where he was crowned king on Christmas Day.

A new England

William took land from English lords and gave it to his Norman followers. He built castles of wood, then stone — including the Tower of London — to hold the country down.

In 1086 his officials recorded every town, farm and taxpayer in England in the Domesday Book. French became the language of the court for centuries, and thousands of French words flowed into English — which is why English has so many French cousins today.

Did You Know?

The Domesday Book was so complete that people named it after Doomsday, the day of judgment — because, like God's judgment, no detail of land or wealth could be hidden from it.
