Abraham Lincoln

This article is written in English. Choose a language to open an automatic translation by Google Translate. Automatic translations can sometimes get historical names and dates wrong — for full accuracy, read the original English article.

Abraham Lincoln was the 16th President of the United States. He led his country through its Civil War and helped to end slavery.

He grew up poor in a log cabin, taught himself law, and became famous for his honesty and his powerful speeches. He was assassinated in 1865, just after the war ended.

From a log cabin to the White House

Lincoln was born in a small log cabin and had very little schooling. He loved books, taught himself, and worked as a shopkeeper, a postmaster and then a lawyer.

People called him Honest Abe. In 1860 he was elected President — just as the southern states were breaking away from the United States over slavery.

The Civil War

The Civil War began in 1861 between the northern Union states and the southern Confederate states. It was the deadliest war in American history.

In 1863 Lincoln issued the Emancipation Proclamation, declaring enslaved people in the Confederate states to be free. Later that year, his short Gettysburg Address became one of the most famous speeches in the English language.

Victory and assassination

In April 1865 the Confederacy surrendered and the war ended. Lincoln wanted to heal the country gently and bring the southern states back with kindness.

Five days later, on 14 April 1865, he was shot while watching a play in Washington by John Wilkes Booth, and died the next morning. The nation he had saved mourned him deeply.

Did You Know?

Lincoln is the only American president to hold a patent. In 1849 he received one for a device to lift boats over shallow water.
