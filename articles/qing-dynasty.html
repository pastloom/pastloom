Empires & Kingdoms

Qing Dynasty

This article is written in English. Choose a language to open an automatic translation by Google Translate. Automatic translations can sometimes get historical names and dates wrong — for full accuracy, read the original English article.

The Qing Dynasty (1644–1912) was China's last imperial dynasty. It was founded by the Manchu people from the north-east, who conquered China and ruled it for 268 years.

Under great emperors like Kangxi and Qianlong, Qing China became the largest and richest empire in its history. But in the 1800s defeats by foreign powers and great rebellions weakened it, and in 1912 the last emperor, the child Puyi, gave up the throne.

The Manchu conquest

As the Ming Dynasty collapsed in rebellion, Manchu armies passed through the Great Wall in 1644 and took Beijing. Within a few decades the Qing ruled all of China.

Manchu men wore the queue hairstyle — a shaved front and long braid — and Han Chinese men were ordered to adopt it as a sign of loyalty. The dynasty kept Chinese officials and Confucian government.

Kangxi, Qianlong and the high Qing

Emperor Kangxi ruled for 61 years — China's longest reign — and was a hard-working ruler who encouraged farming, learning and map-making. His grandson Qianlong expanded the empire into Central Asia, Tibet and Taiwan.

At its height the Qing Empire held over 400 million people and produced famous porcelain, silk and tea for world markets. European traders came to Canton (Guangzhou) to buy Chinese goods.

Decline, rebellion and 1912

In the 1800s Britain defeated China in the Opium Wars, forcing open ports and taking Hong Kong. The Taiping Rebellion (1850–1864) killed tens of millions — one of the deadliest wars in history.

Reform came too slowly. In 1911 revolution broke out, and on 12 February 1912 the six-year-old emperor Puyi abdicated. China became a republic, ending more than 2,000 years of imperial rule.

Did You Know?

The Qianlong Emperor wrote over 40,000 poems in his lifetime — more than all the poems in the famous Tang Dynasty collection combined.
