History of Physics

Physics is the science that asks how the world works: why things fall, how light travels, and what matter and energy really are. It began with people watching the Sun, the Moon and falling stones, and trying to find the rules behind them.

Step by step, physics gave us the laws of motion, the theory of electricity, relativity and the quantum world — and with them, much of the technology of modern life.

Watching the heavens

The first physicists were sky-watchers. In Babylon and Egypt, priests tracked the movements of the Sun, Moon and planets so carefully that they could predict eclipses. Greek thinkers went further: Archimedes worked out the rules of levers and floating bodies.

In the Islamic Golden Age, scholars such as Ibn al-Haytham studied light with real experiments, testing ideas instead of only debating them. For centuries, though, most natural philosophy was still argument rather than measurement.

The great revolution

In the 1600s everything changed. Galileo rolled balls down slopes, dropped weights and pointed a telescope at the sky, showing that the Earth moves and that falling objects follow simple mathematical rules.

In 1687, Isaac Newton's Principia set out his laws of motion and universal gravity — one set of rules for a falling apple and for the Moon alike. In the 1800s, scientists tamed heat and energy, and in 1865 James Clerk Maxwell joined electricity, magnetism and light into a single theory. Physics seemed almost complete.

The world gets stranger

Then, in 1905, a young clerk named Albert Einstein published papers that remade physics: his theory of relativity showed that space and time bend and stretch, and that energy and matter are two forms of one thing.

In the 1920s, Niels Bohr, Werner Heisenberg, Erwin Schrödinger and others built quantum mechanics, in which tiny particles behave like waves and some things can never be known exactly. These strange ideas led to atomic science, lasers, computers and GPS — and physicists are still searching for the deeper rules beneath them.

Did You Know?

Einstein published his first relativity paper in 1905 while working as a clerk in a Swiss patent office — a year so full of great papers that it is still called his 'miracle year'.
