Richard the Lionheart

This article is written in English. Choose a language to open an automatic translation by Google Translate. Automatic translations can sometimes get historical names and dates wrong — for full accuracy, read the original English article.

Richard the Lionheart was King of England from 1189 to 1199, famous across Europe for his courage in battle. He is best known for leading the Third Crusade to the Holy Land, where he fought the great Muslim sultan Saladin.

Richard was a brilliant soldier but spent very little time in England — only about six months of his whole reign. Stories of his age later grew around the legend of Robin Hood.

A warrior prince

Richard was the son of King Henry II and Eleanor of Aquitaine, one of the most powerful women of the age. Raised largely in France, he grew up speaking French and writing poetry — and training for war.

He rebelled against his own father more than once before becoming king in 1189. Almost at once, he began preparing for the great adventure of his life: the Crusade.

The Third Crusade

Jerusalem had fallen to Saladin in 1187, and kings across Europe swore to win it back. Richard sailed east, conquered Cyprus on the way, and in the Holy Land won the battles of Arsuf and Jaffa.

He marched to within sight of Jerusalem twice but knew he could not hold the city. In 1192 he made a peace treaty with Saladin: the coast stayed in Christian hands, and pilgrims could visit Jerusalem freely. The two enemies respected each other's honour and courage.

Captured on the way home

Travelling home in disguise, Richard was captured in Austria by a duke he had quarrelled with, and handed to the Holy Roman Emperor. England had to raise an enormous ransom — 150,000 marks of silver — to free him in 1194.

He forgave the brother who had plotted against him, then spent his last years fighting in France. In 1199 he was wounded by a crossbow bolt at a small castle siege and died, aged forty-one.

Did You Know?

When Richard died, his body was divided between three places: his heart was buried in Rouen, his body beside his father at Fontevraud Abbey, and the rest at Chalus, where he was wounded.
