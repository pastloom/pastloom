History of Deserts

This article is written in English. Choose a language to open an automatic translation by Google Translate. Automatic translations can sometimes get historical names and dates wrong — for full accuracy, read the original English article.

A desert is a land where very little rain falls — usually less than 25 centimetres in a whole year. Deserts cover about one fifth of the Earth's land.

Deserts are not all hot sand. There are cold deserts, stony deserts and coastal deserts — and the largest desert of all is covered in ice. Yet people, plants and animals have found ways to live in them all.

What makes a desert

Deserts form where dry air sinks, where mountains block the rain, or far from the sea. Some, like the Sahara and Arabia, bake under the sun; others, like the Gobi in Asia, freeze in winter.

Life adapts wonderfully. Cactus plants store water, desert foxes hunt by night, and seeds can sleep in the sand for years, bursting into flower after a single rain.

Peoples of the dry lands

Humans have lived in deserts for thousands of years. Nomad peoples moved with their animals between wells and pastures, and traders crossed the dry lands with caravans, resting at oases.

Deserts guarded treasures and faiths: Silk Road cities rose at desert springs, gold crossed the Sahara, and prophets and hermits went into the desert to pray. The driest lands have shaped trade, religion and art far beyond their borders.

Deserts grow and shrink

Deserts are not fixed. The Sahara was green 10,000 years ago, and its edge still moves with the rains. Today, overuse of land and a warming climate are turning some farmland into desert — a problem called desertification.

To fight it, countries in Africa are planting a Great Green Wall of trees across the continent's dry belt. The future of the deserts is, in part, in human hands.

Did You Know?

Antarctica is the largest desert in the world. A desert is defined by how little rain or snow falls — and parts of Antarctica have had almost no precipitation for millions of years.
