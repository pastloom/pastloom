The Hittites

This article is written in English. Choose a language to open an automatic translation by Google Translate. Automatic translations can sometimes get historical names and dates wrong — for full accuracy, read the original English article.

The Hittites were an ancient people who built a powerful empire in Anatolia — modern Turkey — between about 1600 and 1180 BCE.

They were rivals of Egypt and Assyria, pioneers in working iron, and makers of the earliest peace treaty that still survives. Then their empire vanished so completely that for thousands of years people knew them only from a few mentions in old texts.

A power in the mountains

The Hittites built their kingdom among the mountains and plains of central Anatolia. Their capital, Hattusa, was ringed by massive stone walls and gates carved with lions and sphinxes.

From there, Hittite kings conquered much of modern Turkey and Syria. Their chariot armies made them one of the great powers of the Bronze Age, alongside Egypt and Babylon.

Kadesh and the first peace treaty

About 1274 BCE, the Hittites fought Pharaoh Ramses II of Egypt at Kadesh in Syria — one of the largest chariot battles in history. Both sides later claimed victory.

About fifteen years later the two empires signed a peace treaty, copies of which survive in both Egyptian carvings and Hittite clay tablets. A copy hangs at the United Nations today as the earliest known peace treaty between great powers.

The lost empire found again

Around 1180 BCE the Hittite empire broke apart in the general collapse of the Bronze Age world, and Hattusa was abandoned. Smaller Hittite states survived in Syria for a few more centuries.

In the 1800s and 1900s archaeologists uncovered Hattusa and thousands of its tablets. Scholars deciphered the Hittite language and were amazed to find it belonged to the same family as English, Greek and Sanskrit.

Did You Know?

The Hittites wrote with the same cuneiform wedge-writing as Babylon — but their language turned out to be a cousin of English, because both belong to the Indo-European family.
