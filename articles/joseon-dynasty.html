Joseon Dynasty

This article is written in English. Choose a language to open an automatic translation by Google Translate. Automatic translations can sometimes get historical names and dates wrong — for full accuracy, read the original English article.

The Joseon Dynasty ruled Korea for more than 500 years, from 1392 to 1897 — one of the longest-lasting dynasties in world history. Its capital was Hanseong, the city we now call Seoul.

Joseon Korea was a Confucian kingdom famous for King Sejong the Great, who gave Korea its own alphabet, Hangul, in 1443 — so that ordinary people, not only scholars, could read and write.

A new kingdom in Korea

General Yi Seong-gye overthrew the old Goryeo kingdom and founded Joseon in 1392. He built a new capital at Hanseong, with palaces, walls and gates planned by Confucian ideas.

Joseon society was guided by Confucian values — respect for parents, elders and learning. Government officials were chosen by examinations, and scholars held high honour.

Sejong and the gift of Hangul

King Sejong the Great loved learning. His scholars improved calendars, astronomy, medicine and printing — Korea already used metal movable type before Europe.

In 1443 Sejong created Hangul, a simple alphabet of 24 letters designed so that, as he wrote, 'a wise man can learn it in a morning, and even a fool in ten days'. It is still Korea's writing system today.

Invasions and the hermit kingdom

In 1592 Japan invaded Korea. On land Joseon and Ming Chinese armies resisted, and at sea Admiral Yi Sun-sin's armoured 'turtle ships' destroyed Japanese fleets — Yi never lost a battle.

After invasions by Japan and the Manchus, Joseon limited contact with the outside world and was called the 'hermit kingdom'. In 1897 the dynasty proclaimed the Korean Empire, but by 1910 Japan had annexed Korea.

Did You Know?

Hangul's letter shapes were designed to copy the shapes the mouth and tongue make when speaking — it is one of the most scientific alphabets ever invented.
