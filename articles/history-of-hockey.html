History of Hockey

This article is written in English. Choose a language to open an automatic translation by Google Translate. Automatic translations can sometimes get historical names and dates wrong — for full accuracy, read the original English article.

Hockey is a family of fast games played with a curved stick — on grass, on indoor floors, and most famously, on ice.

Field hockey is one of the oldest ball games in the world, while ice hockey was born in the frozen winters of Canada and became that country's great passion.

Sticks and balls through the ages

Pictures from ancient Egypt, some 4,000 years old, show players with curved sticks and a ball — hockey's family is very old. Greek, Persian and Native American peoples all played their own stick-and-ball games.

In cold northern lands, games on frozen ponds were a winter joy for centuries. But it was in Canada that these winter games were organised into a new sport.

Ice hockey — Canada's game

In 1875, students in Montreal played the first organised indoor ice hockey game, with written rules, wooden puck and teams of nine. The game raced across Canada's frozen country, and in 1893 the Governor General, Lord Stanley, gave the Stanley Cup — still the prize of North America's NHL.

Ice hockey spread to Europe and the Soviet Union, becoming a Winter Olympic highlight since 1920. Wayne Gretzky, 'The Great One', rewrote the record books in the 1980s and 90s.

Field hockey around the world

Field hockey took its modern shape in English schools and clubs in the 1800s and joined the Olympics in 1908. British soldiers carried it to India and Pakistan, where it became a national passion.

The Indian wizard Dhyan Chand led his country to Olympic golds in 1928, 1932 and 1936, scoring goals that seemed like magic. Today field hockey is played in more than 100 countries, on watered artificial turf that makes the ball fly faster than ever.

Did You Know?

Dhyan Chand's stick work was so magical at the 1936 Berlin Olympics that, legend says, officials once broke his stick to check whether it hid a magnet.
