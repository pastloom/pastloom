The Lost City of Atlantis

This article is written in English. Choose a language to open an automatic translation by Google Translate. Automatic translations can sometimes get historical names and dates wrong — for full accuracy, read the original English article.

Atlantis is the most famous lost land in the world: a rich island empire that, according to the Greek philosopher Plato, sank beneath the sea in a single day and night, thousands of years ago.

It is important to be clear: Atlantis is a famous story, not a proven place. No writer before Plato mentions it, and no trace of it has ever been found. But the story itself has a real history — people have searched for Atlantis for over 2,000 years, and that search is a fascinating part of history in its own right.

Plato tells the story

In Plato's telling, priests in Egypt described Atlantis to the Greek lawmaker Solon: an island bigger than Libya and Asia together, rich, powerful and proud, which attacked the ancient world before the sea swallowed it.

Plato used Atlantis as a warning. His ideal, humble Athens defeats the proud empire — and then the gods punish Atlantis for its greed. It reads like a lesson dressed as history, which is what most historians believe it was.

Two thousand years of searching

Later ages took the tale literally. Renaissance sailors wondered if America was Atlantis. In 1882 Ignatius Donnelly's bestseller Atlantis: The Antediluvian World argued it was the mother of all civilisations, and the modern hunt exploded.

Since then Atlantis has been 'found' in the Atlantic, the Bahamas, Spain, Sweden, Antarctica and dozens of other places. Every claim has failed the same test: no ancient object, building or writing from Atlantis has ever been produced.

What historians think today

Scholars treat Atlantis as Plato's invention — perhaps seasoned with real memories, such as the eruption of Santorini, which shattered the island of Thera and battered the Minoan world of Crete around 1600 BCE.

The legend survives because it is a perfect story: wealth, pride and a whole world gone in a night. Atlantis may never have existed — but as an idea, it is unsinkable.

Did You Know?

Plato never finished the Critias. The text breaks off just as Zeus rises to pronounce judgement on Atlantis — so even the inventor of the story left its ending untold.
