Joan of Arc

This article is written in English. Choose a language to open an automatic translation by Google Translate. Automatic translations can sometimes get historical names and dates wrong — for full accuracy, read the original English article.

Joan of Arc was a teenage girl from a small French village who led French soldiers in the Hundred Years' War. She said she heard voices from God telling her to save France.

Joan helped lift the siege of Orleans in 1429 and helped Charles VII become king. She was captured, put on trial by the English, and burned at the stake in 1431, when she was only about 19. Today she is a saint and a national hero of France.

Voices and a mission

Joan grew up in Domremy, a farming village, during a long war between France and England. Much of northern France was held by the English, and the French king had not yet been crowned.

From about the age of 13, Joan said she heard the voices of saints telling her to drive the English out and take the Dauphin Charles to Reims to be crowned. At 17 she persuaded local officials to take her to Charles.

The Maid of Orleans

Charles gave Joan armour, a banner and a place with his army. In May 1429 she rode with French forces to Orleans, a key city under English siege for months.

Within days the siege was broken. Joan was wounded by an arrow but returned to the fight. Victory followed victory, and in July 1429 Charles was crowned King Charles VII in Reims Cathedral, with Joan standing nearby.

Trial, death and sainthood

In 1430 Joan was captured by Burgundian soldiers allied with England and sold to the English. She was put on trial in Rouen, accused of heresy and of wearing men's clothes, which she wore as armour and for safety.

She was burned at the stake on 30 May 1431. About 25 years later a new trial cleared her name. In 1920 the Catholic Church made her a saint, and she remains one of the most famous figures in French history.

Did You Know?

Joan could not read or write well, so she dictated her letters — yet at her trial her careful answers impressed even the judges who questioned her for weeks.
