The Search for El Dorado

This article is written in English. Choose a language to open an automatic translation by Google Translate. Automatic translations can sometimes get historical names and dates wrong — for full accuracy, read the original English article.

El Dorado — 'the Golden One' — began as a story about a king covered in gold dust, who washed the gold away in a sacred lake. Spanish conquerors turned the story into a whole city, then a whole kingdom, of gold hidden somewhere in South America.

For more than a hundred years, expeditions marched into jungles and mountains hunting for it. They never found the golden city — but their searches mapped a continent and destroyed much of what they touched.

The golden man of Lake Guatavita

High in the Andes of Colombia, the Muisca people chose their new chief with a shining ceremony. Covered head to foot in sticky gold dust, he sailed to the middle of Lake Guatavita on a raft and offered gold figures and emeralds to the gods of the water, while crowds threw in more gold from the shore.

Spanish soldiers heard the story as they conquered the region — and in their telling, one golden man grew into a golden city, then an empire of gold waiting somewhere beyond the mountains.

Conquerors chase the legend

Quesada's men reached the Muisca plateau in 1537, half-starved, and found real gold — but nothing like the fortune of the legend. Expedition after expedition followed: into the eastern plains, down the Amazon under Orellana, and on Lope de Aguirre's mad, murderous journey of 1560.

Thousands died of hunger, disease and war in these hunts. The peoples of the forests and mountains suffered worst of all, as the search for gold became an excuse for conquest.

El Dorado moves — and fades

Each time one region was emptied of hope, the legend simply moved. In the 1600s mapmakers placed El Dorado beside a great Lake Parime in Guiana, and maps showed that imaginary lake for two hundred years.

Men even tried to drain Lake Guatavita itself; a notch cut in its rim in the 1580s can still be seen, and a company that finally drained it in 1909 found the mud baked too hard to dig. Today 'El Dorado' is a phrase used everywhere for a wonderful prize that can never quite be reached.

Did You Know?

A small golden model raft showing the Muisca ceremony was found hidden in a cave in Colombia in 1969. You can see it today, glowing in the Gold Museum in Bogotá — real proof of the ritual that started the legend.
