Ramesses the Great

This article is written in English. Choose a language to open an automatic translation by Google Translate. Automatic translations can sometimes get historical names and dates wrong — for full accuracy, read the original English article.

Ramesses II, called Ramesses the Great, ruled Egypt for 66 years — so long that many of his subjects were born and died within his reign. He died at about 90 years old, an astonishing age for the ancient world.

He was a warrior who fought the famous Battle of Kadesh, a peacemaker who signed the world's earliest surviving peace treaty, and the greatest builder of all the pharaohs.

Warrior and peacemaker

As a young king, Ramesses marched north to fight the Hittite Empire at Kadesh in Syria. The battle nearly ended in disaster, and Ramesses had to fight his way out with great courage.

Years later, the two empires signed a peace treaty — the earliest peace treaty in the world whose text still survives. A copy of it hangs today at the headquarters of the United Nations.

The greatest builder

Ramesses covered Egypt with his name. He carved the giant temples of Abu Simbel, built a new capital city called Pi-Ramesses, finished the Great Hypostyle Hall at Karnak and raised statues of himself everywhere — some carved from single stones weighing over 80 tonnes.

He also happily carved his name onto older kings' statues, which is why Egyptians today joke that Ramesses was history's first master of self-promotion.

A very long life

Ramesses outlived many of his own children — his thirteenth son finally succeeded him. By the time he died, Egyptians who had known no other king thought the world itself might end.

His mummy, found in 1881, shows a tall man with red hair. In 1976 it was flown to Paris for treatment — and was issued its own Egyptian passport, listing its occupation as 'King (deceased)'.

Did You Know?

Ramesses II's mummy travelled to Paris in 1976 with a real passport — making him perhaps the only pharaoh ever to fly abroad as a legal traveller.
