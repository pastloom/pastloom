World War II

This article is written in English. Choose a language to open an automatic translation by Google Translate. Automatic translations can sometimes get historical names and dates wrong — for full accuracy, read the original English article.

World War II was the biggest and deadliest war in history. It was fought from 1939 to 1945, on land, sea and air, across Europe, Africa, Asia and the Pacific Ocean.

More than 70 million people died, including millions of civilians. The war ended with the defeat of Nazi Germany and Imperial Japan, and it reshaped the whole world.

How the war began

After World War I, Germany was left angry and poor. Adolf Hitler and his Nazi Party took power, promising to make Germany great again, and began taking land from neighbours.

When Germany invaded Poland in September 1939, Britain and France declared war. The Soviet Union joined the Allies after Germany attacked it in 1941, and the United States joined after Japan attacked Pearl Harbor the same year.

A war around the world

Battles raged from the deserts of North Africa to the snows of Russia and the islands of the Pacific. Cities were bombed from the air, and whole peoples suffered terribly.

The Nazis murdered six million Jews in the Holocaust, along with millions of other victims. Remembering this crime is a central lesson of the war.

The end and the new world

In May 1945, Germany surrendered after Soviet and Allied armies met in Berlin. Japan surrendered in September 1945, after atomic bombs were dropped on Hiroshima and Nagasaki.

After the war, the United Nations was created to keep peace, and the world split into two rival camps led by the United States and the Soviet Union — the start of the Cold War.

Did You Know?

One Japanese soldier, Hiroo Onoda, hid in the jungle and did not surrender until 1974 — he had not believed that the war had ended in 1945.
