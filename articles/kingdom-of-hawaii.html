The Kingdom of Hawaii

This article is written in English. Choose a language to open an automatic translation by Google Translate. Automatic translations can sometimes get historical names and dates wrong — for full accuracy, read the original English article.

The Hawaiian Islands are a chain of volcanic islands in the middle of the Pacific Ocean. For centuries each island — and sometimes parts of one island — had its own chiefs, who often fought each other.

In 1795 a powerful chief named Kamehameha began uniting the islands by war and diplomacy. By 1810 he ruled them all, and the Kingdom of Hawaii was born — a Polynesian kingdom that the great powers of the world officially recognised.

Islands of chiefs

Polynesian voyagers settled Hawaii many centuries ago, and over time its people formed chiefdoms led by ali'i (chiefs). The islands of Hawaii, Maui, Oahu and Kauai were separate kingdoms that traded and fought.

In 1778 Captain James Cook arrived — the first European known to reach Hawaii. Foreign ships brought guns, goods and new diseases, and the balance of power between the chiefs began to change.

Kamehameha unites the islands

Kamehameha, a chief from the island of Hawaii, won the Battle of Moku'ohai in 1782 and slowly conquered his rivals. In 1795 his warriors won the fierce Battle of Nu'uanu on Oahu, bringing most islands under one rule.

In 1810 the chief of Kauai accepted Kamehameha's rule peacefully, completing the union. The kingdom made laws, collected taxes and traded sandalwood and supplies with ships from around the world.

The fall of the kingdom

In 1843 Britain and France formally recognised Hawaiian independence, and the kingdom signed treaties with many nations. But foreign settlers gained land and power, and in 1887 the king was forced to accept a constitution that weakened him.

In 1893 a group of American and European businessmen, helped by US forces, overthrew Queen Liliuokalani. The United States annexed Hawaii in 1898, and it became the 50th US state in 1959. Many Native Hawaiians still honour the kingdom's memory and call for respect for their rights.

Did You Know?

The flag of Hawaii is the only US state flag that includes Britain's Union Jack — a sign of the kingdom's old friendship with Britain.
