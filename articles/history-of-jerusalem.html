History of Jerusalem

This article is written in English. Choose a language to open an automatic translation by Google Translate. Automatic translations can sometimes get historical names and dates wrong — for full accuracy, read the original English article.

Jerusalem is one of the oldest and most fought-over cities on Earth. People have lived there for about 5,000 years, and it is a holy city for three great religions: Judaism, Christianity and Islam.

Because so many peoples and faiths cherish it, Jerusalem has been besieged, captured and rebuilt again and again. Its old walled city holds sacred places that draw millions of pilgrims every year.

The ancient city

Jerusalem began as a small hill town near a spring of water. About 1000 BCE, King David made it the capital of his kingdom, and his son Solomon built the First Temple there, the centre of Jewish worship.

Babylonians destroyed the Temple in 586 BCE and carried many people into exile. Later the Temple was rebuilt, and King Herod made it grander still. In 70 CE the Romans destroyed the Second Temple; its Western Wall still stands and is a place of prayer.

Crusades and sultans

In 637 CE Jerusalem came under Muslim rule. The golden Dome of the Rock and the Al-Aqsa Mosque were built on the Temple Mount, a place holy in Islam as the site of the Prophet Muhammad's Night Journey.

Crusaders from Europe captured the city in 1099, but in 1187 the Muslim leader Saladin retook it and allowed people of all faiths to worship. From 1517 to 1917 Jerusalem was part of the Ottoman Empire, and Sultan Suleiman rebuilt the walls that still surround the Old City.

Jerusalem in modern times

After the First World War, Britain ruled the city. In 1948 it was divided between Israel and Jordan, and in 1967 Israel captured the eastern part, including the Old City. Its final status remains one of the hardest questions in world politics.

Today Jerusalem is a living city of nearly a million people. Inside the old walls, the Jewish, Christian, Muslim and Armenian quarters sit side by side — a small map of the city's long, layered history.

Did You Know?

The walls around Jerusalem's Old City that visitors see today were built in the 1500s by the Ottoman Sultan Suleiman the Magnificent.
