The History of Rockstar Games

This article is written in English. Choose a language to open an automatic translation by Google Translate. Automatic translations can sometimes get historical names and dates wrong — for full accuracy, read the original English article.

Rockstar Games is the New York company behind Grand Theft Auto and Red Dead Redemption — the most famous name in open-world games. It was founded in December 1998 by five men from the music and games business, including brothers Sam and Dan Houser.

Rockstar works like no other game company: it says almost nothing for years at a time, then releases one enormous game that the whole world talks about.

From a music label to a games legend

Rockstar's roots lie in BMG Interactive, the games arm of a music company. When Take-Two Interactive bought BMG Interactive's assets in March 1998, it gained the rights to a cheeky Scottish game called Grand Theft Auto, made by DMA Design in Dundee. Three BMG men — Sam Houser, Dan Houser and Jamie King — moved to New York, together with Terry Donovan from the Arista record label and Gary Foreman.

In December 1998 they founded Rockstar Games as Take-Two's bold, high-end label. The name was the programme: they would treat games like rock music — loud, stylish and a little dangerous — and they bet the company's future on rebuilding GTA in full 3D.

The empire of open worlds

The bet paid off beyond all dreams. GTA III (2001), Vice City (2002) and San Andreas (2004) made Rockstar the defining game maker of its age, and DMA Design was renamed Rockstar North in 2002. Around GTA grew other beloved worlds: the stylish shooter Max Payne, the street-racing Midnight Club, the schoolyard adventure Bully, and the Western epic Red Dead Redemption (2010).

Grand Theft Auto V (2013) was reported to have earned $1 billion in three days, and Red Dead Redemption 2 (2018) — a vast, slow, beautiful cowboy story made by Rockstar's studios around the world working as one — showed the company could move players to tears as easily as it made them laugh.

The quiet giant

Rockstar is famous for silence. It rarely advertises between games, seldom attends trade shows, and lets its trailers — watched hundreds of millions of times — do the talking. Dan Houser, the writer behind much of GTA and Red Dead, left the company in 2020; his brother Sam remains its president.

In November 2026 the silence broke again with Grand Theft Auto VI, arriving after the longest wait in gaming history. Nearly thirty years after five men borrowed a name from rock music, Rockstar remains the studio the whole industry watches.

Did You Know?

Rockstar's founders came from the music business — and it shows. GTA's radio stations, filled with real songs and joking DJs, turned the games into giant jukeboxes, introducing millions of players to music made decades before they were born.
