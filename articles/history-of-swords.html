History of Swords

This article is written in English. Choose a language to open an automatic translation by Google Translate. Automatic translations can sometimes get historical names and dates wrong — for full accuracy, read the original English article.

The sword is one of the oldest and most famous weapons in history. For more than 3,000 years, soldiers, knights, samurai and kings carried swords into battle.

A sword is a long, sharp metal blade with a handle. As metals and skills improved, swords changed — from short bronze blades to the long steel swords of knights and the curved katana of Japan.

Bronze and iron blades

The first true swords were cast in bronze about 3,600 years ago. Bronze was soft, so these swords had to be short and thick to survive a fight.

When iron and then steel became common, blades could be longer, stronger and sharper. The Roman gladius, a short iron sword, helped Rome's legions conquer a vast empire.

Swords of knights and samurai

In medieval Europe, knights carried long steel swords that could cut and thrust against armoured enemies. A sword was also a symbol of honour — kings still touch shoulders with a sword when they make a new knight.

In Japan, swordsmiths folded and hammered steel again and again to make the katana, a curved blade so sharp and strong that fine examples are treasured as works of art today.

The sword retires

Guns slowly pushed swords off the battlefield. Cavalrymen kept light curved sabres into the 1900s, but rifles and machine guns ended the sword's long reign.

Swords never fully disappeared. Officers carry them in parades, fencers duel with them in sport, and museums guard famous blades once held by kings and heroes.

Did You Know?

Some Viking swords carry the maker's name +VLFBERH+T (Ulfberht) inlaid in the blade — a famous brand mark more than 1,000 years old.
