History of Morocco

This article is written in English. Choose a language to open an automatic translation by Google Translate. Automatic translations can sometimes get historical names and dates wrong — for full accuracy, read the original English article.

Morocco sits at the north-west corner of Africa, just across the sea from Spain. Its first people were the Amazigh — often called Berbers — who have lived there for thousands of years.

Phoenicians, Romans, Arab dynasties and European powers all shaped Morocco. Its city of Fes holds one of the oldest universities in the world, founded in 859.

Amazigh lands, Phoenicians and Rome

The Amazigh peoples farmed and herded in Morocco long before written history. Phoenician sailors from the east founded trading posts on its coast, and Carthage and then Rome followed.

Rome ruled northern Morocco for about 400 years — the ruins of Volubilis, with its fine mosaics, can still be visited. When Rome fell, Amazigh kingdoms took back the land.

The Islamic dynasties

In the 700s Islam arrived with Arab armies, and Morocco's own dynasties soon rose. The Almoravids, desert Amazigh reformers, founded Marrakech in 1070 and ruled an empire reaching into Spain. The Almohads after them built an even bigger empire and raised the Koutoubia Mosque.

Later dynasties fought off Portuguese and Spanish attacks and traded across the Sahara for gold and salt. Morocco was one of the first countries to recognise the new United States, in 1777.

Protectorate and the modern kingdom

In 1912 France and Spain divided Morocco into protectorates, though the Moroccan sultan stayed on the throne. Resistance grew, and in 1956 Morocco won back full independence.

Today Morocco is a kingdom ruled by King Mohammed VI. It is famous for its old medinas, its mountains and deserts, its mint tea and tagines — and in 2022 its football team became the first from Africa to reach a World Cup semi-final.

Did You Know?

The University of al-Qarawiyyin in Fes, founded in 859 by a woman named Fatima al-Fihri, is often called the oldest university in the world that is still operating.
