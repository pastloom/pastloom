Sacagawea

This article is written in English. Choose a language to open an automatic translation by Google Translate. Automatic translations can sometimes get historical names and dates wrong — for full accuracy, read the original English article.

Sacagawea was a young Shoshone woman who guided the Lewis and Clark Expedition across North America in 1804 to 1806. She travelled thousands of miles carrying her baby son on her back.

Sacagawea translated, found food, saved precious supplies, and her presence with a baby showed other peoples that the explorers came in peace. She is one of the most famous women in American history.

Stolen from her people

Sacagawea was born among the Lemhi Shoshone near the Rocky Mountains. When she was about 12, a Hidatsa raiding party captured her and took her east to villages in present-day North Dakota.

There she became the wife of Toussaint Charbonneau, a French-Canadian fur trader. In the winter of 1804, Captains Meriwether Lewis and William Clark hired Charbonneau as an interpreter — and Sacagawea came too, because she spoke Shoshone.

Guide across a continent

The expedition set out to cross America to the Pacific Ocean. Sacagawea recognised landmarks of her childhood, gathered edible roots and berries, and in one crisis rescued journals and instruments when a boat nearly capsized.

When the explorers needed horses to cross the mountains, they met a Shoshone band led by Sacagawea's own brother, Cameahwait. The emotional reunion helped the expedition gain horses and a guide.

To the ocean and back

In November 1805 the party reached the Pacific. Sacagawea insisted on seeing the ocean and a beached whale — and Clark agreed, writing that she had travelled so far that she deserved it.

The expedition returned safely in 1806. Records suggest Sacagawea died in 1812, though some oral traditions say she lived much longer. Today her image appears on a US dollar coin, with her baby on her back.

Did You Know?

Sacagawea has more statues in the United States than any other American woman.
