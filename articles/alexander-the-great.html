Alexander the Great

This article is written in English. Choose a language to open an automatic translation by Google Translate. Automatic translations can sometimes get historical names and dates wrong — for full accuracy, read the original English article.

Alexander the Great was a king of Macedon, in northern Greece, who built one of the largest empires of the ancient world before he was 33 years old.

In only about 12 years he conquered Greece, Egypt, the Persian Empire and lands as far as India — and he never lost a battle.

A young king

Alexander's father, King Philip, had made Macedon strong. When Philip was killed in 336 BCE, Alexander became king at just 20 years old.

He had been taught by Aristotle, one of the greatest thinkers of Greece, and he carried a copy of the Greek hero-story the Iliad with him on campaign.

Conquering an empire

Alexander first defeated the Persian Empire, the superpower of his age, winning great battles at Issus and Gaugamela. Persia's king Darius fled, and Alexander took his lands.

In Egypt he was welcomed as a pharaoh and founded the city of Alexandria, which became a famous centre of learning. His army marched on to India before his tired soldiers asked to turn back.

Death and legacy

In 323 BCE, Alexander fell ill and died in Babylon, only 32 years old. No one knows the exact cause — fever, illness or poison have all been suggested.

His generals divided the empire between them, but Greek language, art and city life spread across the Middle East for centuries because of his conquests.

Did You Know?

Alexander named more than 20 cities after himself. The most famous, Alexandria in Egypt, held the greatest library of the ancient world.
