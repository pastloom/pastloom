The Spread of Islam in History

This article is written in English. Choose a language to open an automatic translation by Google Translate. Automatic translations can sometimes get historical names and dates wrong — for full accuracy, read the original English article.

Islam began in Arabia in the early 600s CE. Within a hundred years, the lands ruled by its followers stretched from Spain to Central Asia — one of the fastest great expansions in history.

But armies were only part of the story. Over the following centuries Islam also spread peacefully with merchants, sailors, scholars and teachers, reaching West Africa, India, and the islands of South-East Asia. Today it is the world's second-largest religion, with about two billion followers.

The first century: Arabia to Spain

After the Prophet Muhammad's death in 632, the first caliphs led Arab armies out of Arabia. They defeated the tired empires of Byzantium and Persia and took Syria, Egypt, Persia and North Africa.

By 711 their forces had crossed into Spain, and by 750 the caliphate reached Central Asia. In most conquered lands, local people kept their own religions at first; conversion to Islam happened slowly, over generations.

The traders' road: Africa and Asia

From about the 800s, Muslim merchants crossed the Sahara with salt and gold, and towns such as Timbuktu in West Africa became Muslim centres of trade and learning.

Sailors and traders carried Islam down the East African coast and across the Indian Ocean to India, Malaysia and Indonesia. Sufi teachers, known for simple living and storytelling, helped the faith take root in villages far from any army.

A world religion

In later centuries, great Muslim-ruled states — Ottoman, Safavid and Mughal — shaped lands from the Balkans to India, while Muslim communities also grew in places they never ruled.

Today Muslims live in nearly every country, most of them in Asia. The spread of Islam is a clear example of how religions travel by many roads at once — conquest, commerce, scholarship and everyday example.

Did You Know?

The world's largest Muslim-majority country is not in the Middle East at all — it is Indonesia, in South-East Asia, where Islam arrived mainly with traders by sea.
