Queen Nzinga of Ndongo

This article is written in English. Choose a language to open an automatic translation by Google Translate. Automatic translations can sometimes get historical names and dates wrong — for full accuracy, read the original English article.

Queen Nzinga was a brilliant queen and warrior of the Ndongo and Matamba kingdoms, in what is now Angola. For almost 40 years in the 1600s, she led the resistance against Portuguese forces trying to conquer her land.

Nzinga was a skilled diplomat as well as a fighter. She made alliances, outwitted her enemies in famous meetings, and kept her kingdoms free for the whole of her long reign.

The famous meeting

In 1622, Nzinga came to the city of Luanda to negotiate with the Portuguese governor. The story says he offered her only a mat on the floor while he sat on a chair — so one of her servants knelt down, and Nzinga sat on his back, meeting the governor as an equal.

She negotiated a peace treaty and was baptised with the name Ana de Sousa. But the Portuguese broke their promises, and war soon returned.

Queen and war leader

When her brother the king died, Nzinga became ruler of Ndongo in 1624. Driven from her capital by the Portuguese and their allies, she conquered the neighbouring kingdom of Matamba and made it her new base.

From Matamba she led guerrilla campaigns for decades, freed people captured for slavery, and welcomed runaway slaves into her armies. For a time she allied with the Dutch, who had seized Luanda from Portugal.

A long reign remembered

In 1656, old and tired of war, Nzinga made a lasting peace with Portugal. She rebuilt her kingdom and ruled until her death in 1663, at about 80 years old.

Today Nzinga is a national hero of Angola. Statues of her stand in Luanda, and her name is remembered wherever people tell stories of resistance to colonisation.

Did You Know?

Nzinga led her armies in person into old age — Portuguese writers, her enemies, praised her courage and cleverness even while fighting her.
